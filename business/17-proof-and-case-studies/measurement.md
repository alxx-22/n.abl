# Measurement — capturing the before, and the after

The single most important document in this folder, and the one with a deadline
attached to something outside our control: **the baseline must be captured
before delivery starts.** Once the old process is gone, the before-figure cannot
be recovered, and everything else in this folder becomes impossible.

---

## 1. Why this cannot wait until afterwards

A reconstructed baseline is a guess with a client's name on it. Three things go
wrong when the before-figure is estimated after the fact:

1. **Memory inflates it.** Ask someone in month three how long the old job took
   and the answer is longer than the timesheet says. Publishing that number is
   publishing a number nobody can defend.
2. **The client cannot verify it**, so they cannot really consent to it, so the
   permission is worth less than it looks.
3. **It removes the one thing that makes value pricing honest.** The quote's
   projection and the case study's outcome must be separately checkable. If both
   come from the same estimate, there is no outcome — only the projection,
   restated.

The baseline is also useful commercially before it is useful as proof: it is the
number the quote is built on, so it has to be collected anyway. This document
just says to write it down properly and keep it.

---

## 2. The baseline sheet

Captured during **Understand**, confirmed during **Recommend**, and signed off
with the quote. One sheet per engagement.

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Client and engagement | Name, date, who supplied the figures |
| The process, described in their words | Verbatim. This becomes "the problem" line in the case study |
| Who does it | Roles, not names, unless names are needed for the arithmetic |
| How long it takes | Hours per occurrence × occurrences per period |
| How the figure was arrived at | Timed, estimated, or taken from a system. **Record which** |
| Loaded hourly cost | Their figure, or an agreed assumption stated as an assumption |
| Current monthly cost | The arithmetic, written out |
| Error rate, if the cost is mistakes | Occurrences per period, cost per occurrence, how it is currently detected |
| Turnaround, if the cost is delay | Current elapsed time, and what the delay costs |
| What the client says the target is | Their words. Not our proposal |
| Expected residual | The part that will still be manual. **Never zero** |
| Expected monthly cost afterwards | The projection. Labelled as a projection |
| Date captured | |
| Client sign-off | Name and date. An email reply is enough |

**"How the figure was arrived at" is the field people skip and the one that
matters most.** A timed figure and a guessed figure are both usable; publishing
a guessed one as though it were timed is not.

## 3. How to ask, without turning it into an audit

The conversation that produces these numbers is part of **Understand**, and it
must feel like interest rather than assessment. The distinction in
`02-brand/brand-promise.md` §4.2 applies here more than anywhere: we are not
establishing whether the problem deserves solving, we are learning how the work
happens.

Questions that produce good figures:

- "Walk me through it — what happens first?"
- "How often does that happen?"
- "Roughly how long does that part take? Is that a normal week or a bad one?"
- "Who else touches it?"
- "When it goes wrong, how do you find out?"
- "If you had to guess, how many hours a month is the whole thing?"
- "Would you be able to check that against something, or is that a feel?"

That last one is the sentence that turns an estimate into a measurement, and it
is friendly rather than sceptical. Most clients like being asked.

**Ask them to time it once.** If the figure is a feel and the job is worth four
figures, ask the client to time the next occurrence. It takes them ten minutes,
it makes the quote defensible, and it is the strongest signal available that the
process is being taken seriously.

## 4. The after-figure

Captured **at least one full cycle after handover**, not at handover.

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Date measured | Must be ≥ one full cycle after go-live |
| Cycles completed | A monthly report needs at least one month |
| Actual time now | Measured, not estimated. By now there is a system that can tell you |
| Actual residual | Compare to the expected residual. If it is higher, say so |
| Monthly cost now | The arithmetic |
| Realised monthly saving | Before minus after |
| Realised annual saving | × 12, rounded **down** |
| Anything that did not work as expected | Recorded honestly. This is where the next engagement's scope comes from |
| Client's own description of the change | Verbatim. Often better than anything we would write |
| Client sign-off on these figures | Name and date |

### Rounding

Round savings **down**, always, to the nearest sensible unit. £5,880 becomes
"about £5,500". Every ambiguity resolves against us — the same rule as
`12-pricing`. A number the client thinks is conservative is a number they will
repeat to somebody else.

### When the after-figure disappoints

It will happen. The residual is larger than expected, or the process changed, or
the saving is real but half the projection.

**Publish it anyway, or publish nothing — never publish the projection instead.**
A modest, true figure is more persuasive to a sceptical owner than an impressive
one, and it is the only kind that survives a reference call. If the result is
genuinely poor, there is no case study, and the useful output is the entry in
"anything that did not work as expected", which is worth more to the business
than the case study would have been.

## 5. Evidence to capture along the way

Cheap at the time, impossible to get later.

- **Before screenshots.** The spreadsheet, the inbox, the manual process mid-
  flight. Take them during Understand. Nobody keeps a photograph of the mess
  they just got rid of.
- **After screenshots.** The dashboard, the automated output, the tidy version.
- **A short screen recording** of the thing working, if the client permits it.
  This is the raw material for a demonstration, which is form 3 in the README's
  weight table and much stronger than prose.
- **The architecture in three sentences**, written while it is fresh.
- **Anything the client says in passing that sounds like a testimonial.** Write
  it down verbatim with the date. Ask permission later; you cannot recover the
  wording if you do not capture it now.

**Redact before storing.** Screenshots of real business data need customer
names, addresses and figures blurred before they go anywhere near this folder or
a proposal. Do it at capture time, not at publication time.
