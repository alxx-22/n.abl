# Niche research: where AI and Web together fit best

Written 24 September 2026 for n.abl (Nottingham NG/DE7, and Alcester/Stratford/Redditch: B49, B50, B80, B95-B98, CV37, WR11). The research used only desk sources. Nobody was contacted and no forms were submitted.

## 1. The answer

**Primary niche: small practices whose product is a written inspection report.** These are building surveyors, structural engineers who do inspections, and fire risk and health-and-safety assessors, with 3-25 staff. For these firms the report *is* what they sell. The write-up takes longer than the visit, and trade sites say the report arrives 3-10 working days after the inspection. The AI job is clear: turn site notes, dictation and photos into a first draft in the firm's own template and wording, which the professional then checks and signs. The Web job is just as clear. Local firms still say "call us for a quote" while national firms with instant quotes and online booking (Homemove, Simplify Structures and others) rank for "structural engineer Stratford-upon-Avon" and similar searches. A reader can see all of this from the website. On 9 March 2026 a new RICS professional standard on AI use came into force. It gives n.abl a timely, non-salesy reason to write, and it creates a governance job (policy, risk register, client disclosure, output checks) that n.abl can do for the firm.

**Secondary niche: independent wedding and event venues**, starting on the Alcester/Stratford side. The problem is the easiest to see of any candidate: PDF brochures, "email us for availability" and no pricing online. It also costs a lot, because each wedding booking is worth tens of thousands of pounds and couples contact several venues at once. The AI job is drafting replies to enquiries from the venue's own facts (packages, prices, availability, FAQs). The Web job is an availability and price checker, show-round booking, and deposits and staged payments. The universe is small, roughly 50-90 independent venues across both territories. SIC codes find them poorly, so the discovery source should be the councils' **approved-premises registers**, not Companies House.

**Volume fallback, not recommended as the lead:** small accountancy practices. They are the most numerous and can pay, but the need is hard to see from their websites and they already get many offers from software vendors (details below).

**Demote the current trades target as an AI+Web lead.** Trades have the volume, but their AI job is weak because certificates are forms, not prose. Their web gap is so common that it does not pick out good prospects, and the vertical apps (Tradify, Commusoft, Joblogic, iCertifi) already cover quoting and certificates. Keep trades for the automation and software offers.

## 3. Method and caveats on counts

- **ONS UK Business Counts 2026** (released 24 Sep 2026), from the NOMIS API, dataset NM_141_1, `employment_sizeband` 0/2/3/4, `legal_status` 0. These are **local units** (sites), so chain branches count too (e.g. estate agency chains, Co-op funerals). NOMIS rounds every cell to the nearest 5, so small cells show as 0 and the 5-49 sums of many small cells are **approximate, probably low** for rare codes. Wychavon includes Droitwich and Pershore (outside territory), and Mansfield, Newark and Ashfield are NG but a long way from Nottingham, so the SW and Notts totals overstate the core. Query: `https://www.nomisweb.co.uk/api/v01/dataset/NM_141_1.data.csv?geography=<LA codes>&industry=<134217728+SIC>&employment_sizeband=0,1,2,3,4,5&legal_status=0&time=latest&measures=20100`.

## 4. Candidate notes, (a)-(g)

### 4.1 Inspection and report practices (PRIMARY)
- **(a) Count:** see the table. There are also 167 RICS-regulated firms within the RICS "Find a Surveyor" radius search for Stratford-on-Avon, which reaches into Leamington, Redditch and Evesham ([ricsfirms.com](https://www.ricsfirms.com/surveyors/GB/Stratford-on-Avon_362)).
- **(b) Size and pay:** practices of 2-15 surveyors or engineers are common. Fees are high per job: a Level 3 survey averages about £629-£900, and ranges from about £600 for a flat to £1,500+ for a period home ([HomeOwners Alliance](https://hoa.org.uk/advice/guides-for-homeowners/i-am-buying/how-much-does-a-house-survey-cost/), [Compare My Move](https://www.comparemymove.com/guides/surveying/level-3-survey-cost)). A structural inspection in Stratford runs about £480-£585 fixed ([Simplify Structures](https://www.simplifystructures.co.uk/structural-inspection/stratford-upon-avon)). A firm doing 20-40 reports a week can justify £2-10k if it saves even an hour per report. **This is my estimate, not a measured figure.**
- **(c) AI job:** notes, dictation and photos go in; a draft comes out in the firm's own template and standard-paragraph library (defects, probable cause, recommendations), with the professional checking and signing. The inspection takes 3-5 hours, but the write-up usually adds 5-10 working days before the client gets the report ([e.surv](https://www.esurv.co.uk/knowledge-base/rics-level-3-surveys-how-long-do-they-take/)). Vendors describe write-ups that "stretch across evenings and weekends" (a vendor claim: [SiteWrite](https://sitewrite.ai/blog/speed-up-rics-home-survey-report-writing/)). A second AI job is triaging incoming enquiries: property type, age, survey level, and whether it is urgent for exchange.
  **Web job:** an instant fee quote from property type, value and bedrooms, then book and pay a deposit online. After that, send access and key-holder details, and deliver the report through a secure link instead of an email attachment.
- **(d) Detectable signals:** see section 6. I checked this on sample sites. A Nottingham RICS surveyor's site gives a phone number and email only, with no instant quote or online booking ([stevebutler.co.uk](https://www.stevebutler.co.uk/)). A Nottingham structural engineer's site gives phone numbers and a personal email for quotes and has no online booking ([jsl-consulting.co.uk](https://www.jsl-consulting.co.uk/)). Meanwhile national operators with instant-quote location pages rank locally ([Homemove Stratford](https://homemove.com/surveys/rics-level-3/stratford-upon-avon/), [Simplify Structures Nottingham](https://www.simplifystructures.co.uk/structural-engineer/nottingham)).
- **(e) Competition:** this is **crowded at the product level**, and the letter must not pretend otherwise.
  - Residential RICS AI report writers: [Otto by Home.co.uk](https://home.co.uk/surveyors/tools), [SurveyorSuite](https://surveyorsuite.co.uk/), [Surveyor AI](https://surveyorai.io/), [SiteWrite](https://sitewrite.ai/).
  - A general inspection-report writer: [SILVER](https://www.silver-ai.co.uk/).
  - Fire risk assessment tools: [FireCheckr](https://firecheckr.co.uk/), [AssessHub](https://assesshub.co.uk/), [InspectReport AI](https://www.inspectreport.ai/), [Risk Assessor Pro](https://www.riskassessorpro.com/).
  - Damp and timber tools: [DampApp Pro](https://dampsurvey.pro/).
  - A surveyor CRM and quote tool: [Survey Booker](https://surveybooker.co.uk/rics-find-a-surveyor-2/).

  **The room left for n.abl** has four parts:
  - These are young, single-purpose apps, and most small firms have not adopted them.
  - Structural engineers, commercial building surveyors (schedules of condition, dilapidations) and H&S consultancies write reports in their own formats that the templated apps fit poorly.
  - None of the apps builds the firm's own website quote → booking → payment → delivery flow.
  - The RICS standard asks firms to do due diligence on AI tools and keep governance records, which is setup and advice work.

  Honest position: sometimes the right AI answer is "configure one of these apps", and n.abl earns on the web flow and the integration.
- **(f) Risk:** professional liability for the content of reports (PI insurance). The [RICS Responsible use of AI professional standard](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/conduct-competence/responsible-use-of-ai) has been mandatory since 9 March 2026. It requires governance and a risk register, surveyor oversight, disclosure to clients and due diligence on AI tools ([RICS](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/rics-first-ever-standard-on-responsible-ai-use-now-in-effect), [Beale & Co](https://beale-law.com/article/rics-sets-the-standard-responsible-ai-use-becomes-mandatory-in-surveying/)). Personal data is moderate: names, addresses and photos inside homes. The design must keep the human sign-off and must not invent observations.
- **(g) SIC:** 71129, 71122, 74909, 74902. Optionally 71200, but it is noisy because MOT test centres sit in it ([siccode.co.uk](https://www.siccode.co.uk/sic2007/code-71200)). Some residential surveyors register as 68310; filter by name ("survey").

### 4.2 Independent wedding and event venues (SECONDARY)
- **(a) Count:** the Warwickshire approved-venue register lists 90 venues. About 22 are in CV37, B49, B95, B98 and WR11, and some of those are chain hotels or trusts ([Warwickshire register](https://apps.warwickshire.gov.uk/ApprovedVenue/)). Nottinghamshire has about 70 approved premises ([Notts CC report / weddings site](https://weddings.nottinghamshire.gov.uk/media/kz1h1lig/weddingbrochure.pdf)) and Nottingham city about 20 ([weddingvenuelist](https://weddingvenuelist.co.uk/venues-in/nottingham), [Nottingham City list](https://nottinghamcity.gov.uk/information-for-residents/births-deaths-marriages/marriages-civil-partnerships-other-ceremonies/approved-premises-for-marriages-and-civil-partnerships)). Worcestershire publishes its own list ([Worcestershire ceremonies](https://www.worcestershire.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-04/civil_ceremonies_in_worcestershire_brochure.pdf)). Barns and marquee sites without a civil licence are extra. **Estimate: 50-90 independent, non-chain venues across both territories.**
- **(b) Size and pay:** a typical independent venue (manor, barn, country hotel) has 5-25 staff plus casual staff, and each booking is worth a lot. Ability to pay is good. Seasonality means the best time to sell is autumn and winter.
- **(c) AI job:** draft the first reply to each enquiry within minutes, grounded in the venue's own packages, prices, availability and FAQ, with a person approving it. Also handle follow-ups after show-rounds and questions about supplier lists and timings.
  **Web job:**
  - A date availability checker and "from" price calculator by guest numbers and season, replacing the brochure PDF behind email.
  - Show-round booking.
  - Deposit and staged-payment collection.
  - An online couple's planning form instead of PDFs.
- **Evidence the need is costly:** a vendor dataset says the average UK venue takes about 47 hours to answer an enquiry, and 68-72% of enquiries arrive out of hours ([VenueBot 2026 report](https://venuebot.io/blog/2026-state-of-uk-wedding-venue-enquiries)). **It comes from a vendor with an interest, so treat it as indicative.**
- **(d) Signals:** see section 6.
- **(e) Competition:** venue CRMs such as BriteBiz, Event Boss, Sonas, Hostology, iVvy, XEPOS, plus AI reply tools like VenueBot ([Guides for Brides CRM guide](https://guidesforbrides.co.uk/business-information/crm-systems-wedding-venues-suppliers), [Event Boss](https://eventboss.co.uk/venue-software)). Listings on Hitched and Bridebook send enquiries through their own channels. There is room for n.abl because most independents run on email, a website and a diary. A local partner who builds the site flow and connects it to the chosen CRM is credible, especially one based in Alcester.
- **(f) Risk:** low. Ordinary customer data and card payments go through a processor. It is not regulated advice.
- **(g) SIC:** poor. Venues sit under 55100, 55209, 56210, 93290, farming codes, or 68209 (letting of own property, which the repo refuses). **Discover from the approved-premises registers**, then match to Companies House for the company.

### 4.3 Accountancy and bookkeeping practices
- **(a)-(b):** the most practices in the 5-49 band (69201 Notts 120, SW 65). They can pay well.
- **(c) AI job:** triage the client inbox and draft replies; pull figures from bank statements, P60s and rental statements. MTD for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026 for income over £50k, which brings quarterly cycles ([TaxCalc](https://www.taxcalc.com/blog/how-ai-is-transforming-uk-accounting-practices-2026)).
  **Web job:** an online fee quote, onboarding with AML/ID, and secure document upload instead of emailed attachments.
- **(d)** Signals are weak because most accountancy sites look the same: "download our new client form (PDF)" and "email us your records" are the best ones.
- **(e)** Crowded: Karbon, Pixie, IRIS, Senta, Dext, AutoEntry, Ignition, GoProposal, and specialist accountancy-website suppliers ([TaxCalc](https://www.taxcalc.com/blog/how-ai-is-transforming-uk-accounting-practices-2026), [Accountancy Age](https://accountancyage.com/2026/09/02/the-quiet-tech-revolution-reshaping-uk-accountancy-in-2026/)).
- **(f)** Financial data, AML duties and confidentiality under professional-body rules.
- **(g)** 69201, 69202 (69203 is small).

### 4.4 Training providers
- **(c) AI job:** issue certificates and joining instructions, answer course-enquiry emails, and triage bespoke on-site course requests.
  **Web job:** public course calendar with online booking and payment. Signals include "call to book" and PDF booking forms.
- **(e)** Arlo, accessplanit and BookitLive already serve H&S and first-aid providers ([Arlo](https://www.arlo.co/health-and-safety-training-software), [accessplanit](https://www.accessplanit.com/), [BookitLive](https://www.bookitlive.net/content/business/first-aid-training-course-booking-software/)).
- **(g)** 85590 is a catch-all (tutoring, language schools and more), so it needs website qualification. A reasonable third preset, weaker on AI.

### 4.5 Trades with field teams (current target)
- **Counts:** the largest (see table).
- **AI job:** thin (quote wording, enquiry triage). Certificates are forms that iCertifi, Joblogic and Commusoft already produce ([HiveSuite roundup](https://www.hivesuite.co.uk/blogs/best-uk-job-management-software-2026), [Checker App](https://checker.app/best-all-in-one-app-electricians-certificates-invoices-uk-2026/)).
- **Web job:** real (quote request and booking), but Checkatrade and MyBuilder capture much of that demand.
- **Detectability:** poor. Many firms have no site, or a site that cannot be reached; the repo's earlier batches show this.
- **Conclusion:** better for automation and job-management integration than for AI+Web.

### 4.6 Estate and letting agents
- **Size and pay:** good pay, and volume is good for 68310.
- **AI jobs:** listing copy, enquiry handling, material information, and the Renters' Rights Act changes. The Act came fully into force on 1 May 2026 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/421/made), [Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/renters-rights-bill-a-letting-agents-guide)).
- **Why it ranks low:** the three main CRMs now ship AI for listings, 24/7 lead handling and call answering ([Alto](https://www.altosoftware.co.uk/blog/the-best-ai-crms-for-estate-agents-in-2026-alto-street-and-reapit-compared/)). Agent websites are CRM-fed products with instant valuation tools from specialist suppliers ([Homeflow](https://www.homeflow.co.uk/products)). A generalist has little room.

### 4.7 Recruitment agencies
- **AI and Web jobs:** CV formatting, job-ad drafting, candidate triage, job boards.
- **Why it ranks low:** at least a dozen CV-formatting AI tools plugged into Bullhorn and Vincere ([FormaCV list](https://formacv.ai/blog/best-cv-formatting-tools), [Allsorter](https://allsorter.com/answers/best-cv-formatting-software-uk)). CVs are personal data at scale. Low room.

### 4.8 Solicitors and conveyancers
- **Web signal:** the SRA Transparency Rules require conveyancing price information on the website ([SRA](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/transparency-rules/)), so a price page or quote calculator is detectable. The market already has quote-engine suppliers ([Quotexpress](https://www.quotexpress.co.uk/sra-transparency-rules-cma)).
- **Why it ranks low:** the SRA regulates them, confidentiality is strict, case-management software is entrenched, and firms are 5+ fee-earners with procurement habits. High risk for a new two-person firm.

### 4.9 Funeral directors
- **Detectable signal:** the CMA Funerals Market Order 2021 requires a "Standardised Price List" PDF no more than one click from the homepage ([Funeral Director Websites](https://www.funeraldirectorwebsites.co.uk/funerals-market-order/)).
- **Why it ranks low:** the AI job is weak and sensitive, specialist web suppliers exist, many local units are chain branches, and the numbers are low (SW about 20 units).

### 4.10 Holiday lets and glamping
- **Why it ranks low:** often run by sole traders or partnerships, not companies, with 1-3 staff. Channel managers and Airbnb/Sykes dominate bookings. England's short-term let register was still not live in mid-2026 ([STL Solutions](https://stlsolutions.co.uk/blog/england-registration-scheme-april-2026)), so a compliance hook may come later. For now, this belongs inside the venues preset only where a site does both weddings and stays.

### 4.11 Driving schools and tutoring
- **Why it ranks low:** mostly sole traders (85530: Notts 35 units, SW 10; none in the 5-49 band after rounding), and national booking apps compete. Not a fit.

## 6. Website signals for the automated reader

### P1 report practices (look for 5-8 of these)
1. **Turnaround stated in days:** "report within 3-5 / 5-7 / 7-10 working days", "reports in 48 hours for an extra fee". This shows the report is the bottleneck.
2. **Quotes by phone or email only:** "call for a quote", "email your address and we'll quote". No instant quote calculator or price table while local rivals have one.
3. **No online booking or payment:** no date picker and no deposit or card payment. Watch for "we'll call you to arrange access".
4. **Reports in their own house style:** a sample report PDF, or several named report types (schedule of condition, dilapidations, specific defect, structural inspection for mortgage). These show a template the SaaS apps may not fit.
5. **Signs of team size:** several named surveyors or engineers, "our team of", or a vacancy for a surveyor, engineer or **report typist/administrator**. A typist vacancy is a strong AI signal.
6. **Accreditation lines:** "RICS Regulated Firm", "IStructE/ICE chartered", "IFSM/FRACS/BAFE SP205", "PCA member". These confirm the niche and professional duties, and are the hook for the AI governance point.
7. **Document handling:** "we will email your report" and a PDF instruction or terms form to print, sign and return.
8. **Out-of-date site:** copyright over two years old, not mobile-friendly, http only, no structured service pages. This is a secondary Web signal.

### P2 venues
1. Brochure or price list only as a PDF, or "request our brochure" behind a form.
2. "Contact us for availability", with no date checker.
3. No "from" prices, or pricing only in the PDF.
4. Show-rounds "by appointment, please call", with no booking link.
5. An enquiry form with many free-text fields, or only an email address, and no instant confirmation.
6. Reviews or wording about slow replies, e.g. "we aim to respond within 48 hours".
7. Deposits "by bank transfer" and no online payment.
8. Listed on Hitched/Bridebook but the site links to the listing for enquiries. This suggests they depend on the directories.

## 7. The opening line of the letter

**Report practices.** Open with their own turnaround and the new standard, not with "AI". For example: *"Your website promises the full report within five working days of the inspection, so I expect the evenings after a busy week of site visits go on writing up, and since March the RICS standard on AI means any shortcut there has to be one you can show your working for."* Then name the combined fix in one sentence: *"We set up drafting from your own site notes, in your own report template, that you check and sign, and we put a fee quote and booking on your site so the enquiries that now go to national firms with instant quotes come to you."* Adapt the professional-body reference by firm type: RICS for surveyors, IStructE or ICE for engineers, the relevant fire-assessor register for fire risk assessors. Quote their own words and stated days from the site. Do not claim clients or results, and do not name competitor apps.

**Venues.** Open with the couple's point of view and their own brochure: *"A couple looking at your website this evening can read about the orchard ceremony, but to find out whether next June is free, or roughly what it costs for 90 guests, they have to email and wait, while they are emailing four other venues too."*

- **Adoption of AI report apps among local firms is unknown.** Some prospects will already use one. The reader should look for "reports produced using [app]" or app-style sample report PDFs.
- **VenueBot's response-time figures come from a vendor.** The Warwickshire venue count is from my postcode filter of the register on 24 Sep 2026 (90 listed; about 22 in the target postcodes).
- **Rounding.** NOMIS rounds cells to 5, and local units include chain branches. Companies House counts match text in the registered office. None of these figures is a headcount.
- **SIC revision.** ONS is revising UK SIC. If Companies House changes code lists, re-verify the presets. I did not confirm a date.
