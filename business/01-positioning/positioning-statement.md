# Positioning statement

The words n.abl uses to describe itself. Everything client-facing derives from
this file: the website, the outreach templates, the proposal covers, the way a
call is opened.

If a sentence about n.abl appears anywhere and contradicts this file, this file
wins and the sentence gets changed.

This file decides **what n.abl is**.
[`messaging-spine.md`](messaging-spine.md) decides **how it gets said** in each
place it has to be said.

Last substantive revision: 2026-08-15.

---

## 1. The statement

**Short form, one line.** Use this on the site, in a signature, in a first
sentence.

> n.abl is a technology implementation partner for small businesses. We find the
> expensive, repetitive or fragile part of your business and build the right fix
> for it.

**Standard form.** Use this on the About page, in a proposal, in a directory
listing.

> n.abl is a technology implementation partner for small businesses in the
> Nottingham and Alcester areas.
>
> We find the part of your business that costs the most time, causes the most
> mistakes or breaks the most often, and we build the most appropriate solution
> for it. That might be automation, a piece of software, a website, an
> integration between two systems that do not talk to each other, or training
> your team on the tools you already pay for.
>
> AI is one of the tools we use. It is not the point. If the best answer to your
> problem is forty lines of ordinary code, you get forty lines of ordinary code,
> and you pay less for it.

**Long form.** Use this where there is room to explain the commercial model, such
as a capabilities document or a longer About page.

> Most small businesses do not have a technology problem. They have a process
> problem, and technology is one of the things that fixes it.
>
> n.abl starts from the process. We look at what a task actually costs you now,
> in hours and in errors, and what it would cost after it is fixed. The gap
> between those two numbers is what we are selling, and we will show you the
> arithmetic before you commit to anything.
>
> We build with whatever is appropriate: ordinary code, web development, system
> integration, automation, AI where it genuinely earns its place, and training
> where the answer is that the tool is fine and the team was never shown how to
> use it.
>
> There are no retainers. You pay once for the implementation. Afterwards you can
> buy credits and spend them when you actually need something, rather than paying
> us monthly to be on standby. You own everything we build.

## 2. The organising question

n.abl does not lead with what it makes. It leads with a question:

> **What are you trying to improve?**

Every piece of client-facing copy, every outreach message and every discovery
call should be structured so a client can answer that question in their own
words, without knowing anything about technology.

The six answers are the six categories in
[`service-categories.md`](service-categories.md):

| Category | The client's own words |
|---|---|
| Save time | "This takes us ages every month" |
| Reduce mistakes | "We keep getting this wrong" |
| Understand your data | "We have the data, but not the answers" |
| Build something new | "We need a thing that does X and nothing off the shelf does it" |
| Train your team | "We pay for this software and nobody really uses it" |
| Fix something | "It worked and now it doesn't" |

That table is the navigation, the sales script and the proposal structure. The
same six in all three places, deliberately.

## 3. What n.abl is not

State these internally with total clarity. Say the softened version out loud, and
the softened versions are written out in [`saying-no.md`](saying-no.md).

**We are not an AI automation agency.** This framing is retired. It is a sentence
thousands of companies can say and none of them can say differently, it collapses
the moment model calls get cheap, and it only addresses businesses that have
already decided they want AI, which is a small and badly-informed slice of the
market.

**We are not a software house taking orders.** A client arriving with a finished
specification and asking for a quote to build it is a lower-quality engagement
than a client arriving with a problem. We can do it, but the diagnosis is where
the value is, and a fixed spec skips it.

**We are not an IT support company.** We do not manage networks, supply hardware,
run helpdesks or take over a Microsoft 365 tenancy. Where a client needs that, we
say so and stay out of the way of whoever provides it.

**We are not a retainer business.** See the master plan, section 3. This is a
structural decision, not a pricing preference, and it is a selling point rather
than a gap.

**We are not consultants.** We do not deliver a report and leave. Every
engagement ends with something built, running and owned by the client.

**We do not supply regulated advice.** Legal, medical, financial. We build the
system. What the system says is the client's responsibility, and that boundary
goes in the scope document every time.

## 4. Why this positioning, in commercial terms

Three reasons, in order of weight. Know them, because a client will occasionally
ask why we do not just say "AI agency" like everyone else.

1. **It is defensible.** Our competitor's pitch is a tool. Ours is an
   understanding of how a specific business loses money. The first can be copied
   in an afternoon. The second cannot be copied at all, because it is built one
   client at a time.

2. **It survives the hype cycle.** The cost of a model call is falling and will
   keep falling. A business that sells model calls is selling something on its
   way to being free. A business that sells "we worked out what your problem
   actually was" still has that when the models are free.

3. **The market is much larger.** Almost no small business in Nottinghamshire or
   Warwickshire wakes up wanting AI. A great many of them have a job that eats a
   day a week. The second group is the market, and it is an order of magnitude
   bigger than the first.

There is a fourth reason worth saying to a client directly, because it builds
trust faster than anything else in the pitch:

> "If AI is not the right answer for your problem, I will tell you, and I will
> build the boring version instead. It will be cheaper and it will break less
> often."

## 5. Innovation / Automation / Optimisation

The three pillars are **brand architecture**. They are what n.abl is remembered
by, alongside the promise: *We make your business work smarter.*

> **INNOVATION** — Find better ways to do things.
> **AUTOMATION** — Take repetitive work off people's hands.
> **OPTIMISATION** — Get more from the people, processes, systems and data you
> already have.

They belong in the strapline, in the band beneath the hero, in the footer, on a
business card, and in the sentence you say when somebody asks what n.abl does
and you have four seconds.

They must **not** be the organising structure of the offer. Not the site
navigation, not the service pages, not the proposal contents page, not the way a
call is opened. Those are the six categories in section 2.

The reason for the split: Innovation / Automation / Optimisation is a
description of our toolbox, arranged for our convenience. A client with a job
that takes twelve hours a month does not know which pillar that is, and asking
them to guess makes them feel stupid at exactly the moment we need them to talk
freely. But the same three words, used as a mnemonic rather than a menu, are the
most memorable thing the business has — and "technology implementation partner",
while accurate, is not something anybody repeats to a friend.

**Current status: done.** The site leads with the promise, carries the pillars
as a brand band under the hero and as the footer strapline, and organises the
offer around the six problem categories. Both halves are in place. The earlier
version of this section said the pillars survived "only as a strapline, if at
all", and that hedge got them deleted entirely in the repositioning. Do not
re-introduce the hedge.

## 6. Proof, and how to talk about it honestly

n.abl is new. There are no case studies, no client names and no testimonials, and
we do not invent any. What we have instead is real and worth using:

- **The software we built for ourselves.** A marketing site, a client portal with
  access-key sign-in and row-level security, an internal team space, a sales CRM,
  six branded email templates and a document generator. Built, deployed and in
  use. This is a portfolio, and it is honest to describe it as one.
- **Security decisions we can point at.** Portal access keys are 59.5-bit and
  generated with a cryptographically secure random number generator. Login is
  rate limited per IP, per key prefix and globally. Storage is private and served
  through short-lived signed URLs. Fonts are self-hosted rather than loaded from
  a font CDN, so visiting the site does not disclose a visitor's IP address to a
  third party. Small businesses are rarely shown this level of care and they
  notice when they are.
- **The arithmetic.** We can price a job in front of a client, on a piece of
  paper, and let them check it. Most people quoting them for technology cannot or
  will not do that.

**What to say when asked "who else have you worked with?"** See
[`objection-handling.md`](objection-handling.md), objection 6. Do not improvise
this one.

meant to read as competence, not as noise.

## 8. Brand rules that affect copy

Full detail lives in `business/02-brand/`. Three rules matter to anyone writing
about n.abl:

1. **The logo is drawn artwork, not type.** It is a 13-unit monoline stroke with
   butt caps and true-circle curves. Masters are `public/brand/wordmark.svg` and
   `public/brand/mark.svg`. Never write that the wordmark is "set in" any
   typeface, in any document, ever. The old pack says exactly this and the old
   pack is wrong.
2. **The name is lower case with a full stop: `n.abl`.** The full stop is a
   square and it is amber. Do not capitalise it to "N.abl" at the start of a
   sentence. Rewrite the sentence instead.
3. **On light backgrounds, amber accent text must be the deep amber `#B87718`.**
   The primary amber `#E9AC57` reaches only 1.97:1 on near-white and fails
   contrast outright. This applies to PDFs, proposals and slides, not just the
   website.

## 10. Review

Revisit this file when any of the following happens:

- The first three paid engagements are complete, and the language clients
  actually used to describe their problems can be fed back into section 2.
- A category in `service-categories.md` turns out to attract only unqualified
  enquiries, or attracts none at all.
- The geography changes. The standard form names two areas and would need
  rewriting if a third is added.

Do not revise it because a single prospect did not understand it. Revise it when
three do.
