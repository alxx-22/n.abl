# Ideal customer profile

Who n.abl sells to, defined tightly enough to throw work away.

It starts with small businesses around **Nottingham** and **Alcester**, and it is
defined by three things in this order: **employee count**, **sector**, and
**observable signals** that can be seen from outside before anyone has spoken to
anyone.

A profile that does not disqualify anyone is not a profile, it is a wish. This
one is built so that a person, or eventually a script, can look at a business
from the outside and decide whether it is worth any time at all.

The words for actually turning someone away are in [`saying-no.md`](saying-no.md).

Last substantive revision: 2026-08-15.

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

> An owner-run business of 5 to 25 people in the Nottingham or Alcester areas,
> running on spreadsheets and email, with at least one process that visibly costs
> them a day a week or more, and a named director who can say yes on their own.

Everything below is that sentence made checkable.

## 2. Geography

Two territories, because that is where n.abl has people on the ground. Being able
to sit in someone's office for an hour is a genuine advantage against a remote
supplier, and the whole first phase of the business is built on it.

### Territory A — Nottingham

**Core.** Nottingham city, Beeston, West Bridgford, Arnold, Carlton, Gedling,
Bulwell, Hucknall, Stapleford, Long Eaton, Ilkeston, Kimberley, Eastwood,
Ruddington, Bingham.

**Edge.** Newark-on-Trent, Mansfield, Sutton-in-Ashfield, Derby, Loughborough.
Take these if the fit is otherwise strong, but do not prospect them
systematically until the core is worked.

### Territory B — Alcester

Alcester is a small market town, so the territory is defined by the towns around
it rather than the town itself.

**Core.** Alcester, Studley, Redditch, Bidford-on-Avon, Henley-in-Arden,
Wootton Wawen, Astwood Bank, Stratford-upon-Avon.

**Edge.** Evesham, Bromsgrove, Warwick, Leamington Spa, Solihull, Wythall,
Inkberrow.

### The rule

Core territory is roughly "can be visited and returned from inside half a day
without it being an event". Edge territory is worth a trip only for a qualified
opportunity.

**Outside both territories is a disqualifier during phase one**, including remote
work for a well-fitting business elsewhere in the UK. This will feel wasteful the
first time a good lead is turned away. Take it anyway. A concentrated territory
produces referrals, recognisable names and a reputation that travels between
businesses that drink in the same places. A scattered client list produces none
of that.

### Where to physically look

Trading estates are the highest-density source of the profile in both
territories. Around Nottingham: Colwick, Blenheim in Bulwell, Lenton, Castle
Marina, Wigwam Lane in Hucknall, and the estates along the A52 and A610
corridors. Around Alcester: the Arden Forest and Kinwarton estates, and the
Redditch estates at Washford, Moons Moat and Ravensbank.

Verify each estate is current and occupied before planning a visit. This list is
a starting point for research, not a verified dataset.

## 3. Size

Measured by employee count, because it is the best available proxy for both the
existence of a process problem and the ability to pay.

| Band | Employees | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Too small | 1 to 2 | **Disqualify**, with one exception below |
| Edge | 3 to 4 | Qualify only on a strong signal and a clear budget |
| **Core** | **5 to 25** | **The target.** Prospect these first. |
| Edge | 26 to 50 | Qualify, but check for an in-house IT person and for procurement |
| Too large | 51+ | **Disqualify** in phase one |

**Why 5 is the floor.** Below roughly five people, a process problem is usually
still inside one person's head and one person's working day. There is rarely a
handover, rarely a written procedure, and rarely four figures of discretionary
spend for something that is not urgent. The owner absorbs the pain personally and
has decided to live with it.

**The exception to the floor.** A one or two person business with high revenue
per head, where the owner's own time is the bottleneck and is demonstrably
valuable. A sole practitioner surveyor, a consultant billing at a real rate, a
two-person agency turning down work. Here the arithmetic in the pricing method
works harder than usual, because the loaded hourly cost being saved is not £20,
it is the owner's own charge-out rate.

**Why 50 is the ceiling.** Above roughly fifty people a business tends to acquire
an IT manager, a preferred supplier list, a security questionnaire and a
purchasing process with more than one signature in it. The overhead of winning
the work starts to exceed the work.

**Turnover as a cross-check.** [PLACEHOLDER — a working band of roughly £250k to
£5m turnover is the expectation, but confirm it against the first ten qualified
conversations before treating it as a filter. Companies House filings for small
companies often do not show turnover, so employee count remains the primary
measure.]

## 4. Sectors

Sector is a weaker signal than the observable signals in section 5, and should
never override them. Its use is to decide where to spend research time first.

### Strong fit, both territories

**Trades and installers with a field team.** Electrical, plumbing and heating,
HVAC, flooring, roofing, scaffolding, fire and security, groundworks. Five to
twenty vans is the sweet spot. Every one of them has the same cluster of
problems: scheduling, job sheets that arrive on paper or as photographs,
quoting from a price list that lives in one person's spreadsheet, certificates
and qualifications with expiry dates, and an invoicing gap of days between the
job finishing and the invoice going out.

**Light manufacturing, engineering and fabrication subcontractors.** Production
scheduling on a whiteboard, quoting from historic job cards, stock counted by
walking round with a clipboard, delivery notes reconciled against invoices by
hand, certificates and calibration records in a folder.

**Wholesale, distribution and trade supply.** Price lists in spreadsheets that
have to be reissued whenever a supplier changes, order entry retyped from
emailed purchase orders, stock figures that are true on Monday and wrong by
Thursday.

**Professional services, small practices.** Accountants and bookkeepers,
surveyors, architects, planning consultants, small solicitors' firms, insurance
brokers, recruitment agencies. Document generation, client onboarding, chasing
information from clients, and deadline tracking. Note the regulated advice
boundary in section 6.

**Property, lettings and facilities management.** Inspection schedules,
compliance certificate expiries, contractor coordination, tenant communication,
statements to landlords compiled by hand each month.

### Additionally strong around Nottingham

Logistics and small haulage operators. Training providers and independent
education. Care agencies and domiciliary care, subject to the data caution in
section 6. Print and signage. Event and equipment hire.

### Additionally strong around Alcester

Agricultural and rural businesses: farm supply, land management, equestrian,
agricultural contracting. Automotive and aerospace supply chain feeding
Redditch, Coventry and Birmingham. Hospitality, tourism, wedding and event
venues around Stratford-upon-Avon, where availability, deposits and enquiry
follow-up are almost always manual. Specialist building trades and restoration
serving a conservation-heavy area.

### Weak fit, deprioritise

Pure retail with no back office. Hairdressing, beauty and salons, where the
sector already has cheap, adequate booking software. Restaurants and pubs, same
reason. Anything single-site and under five people serving walk-in trade.

## 5. Observable signals

This is the operative part of the profile. Each signal is visible from outside
the business, before any contact, from a website, a Companies House record, a job
advert or a van.

Signals beat sector. A signal-rich business in a weak-fit sector is a better
prospect than a signal-free business in a strong-fit one.

### Tier 1 — strong signals, each worth acting on alone

- **A downloadable form that has to be printed, filled in by hand and emailed
  back.** A booking form, a credit account application, a data sheet, a job
  request. This is a process with a hole in the middle of it and the hole is
  filled by a person retyping things.
- **"Call us for a quote" or "please allow 48 hours for a quote" on a product or
  service that is obviously standardised.** The delay is someone assembling a
  number by hand.
- **A repeatedly advertised administrator, office administrator or data entry
  role.** Either they cannot retain the person, or the job is unpleasant enough
  that it keeps being vacant. Both mean the work is mechanical.
- **A job advert for an operations role requiring "advanced Excel" or "strong
  Excel skills".** The business is running an operational system inside a
  spreadsheet and knows it.
- **Two systems that visibly do not talk to each other.** A booking widget from
  one vendor, an invoicing product mentioned somewhere else, a separate
  newsletter tool. Someone is the integration.
- **Reviews mentioning slow responses**, "never got back to me", "had to chase",
  "rang three times". Public evidence that something between an enquiry arriving
  and somebody answering it is broken — usually a "save time" or "fix something"
  job once you get inside it.
- **A price list published as a dated PDF.** The date is the version control.

### Tier 2 — supporting signals, meaningful in combination

- Contact page with a bare email address and no form.
- Generic email domain on the website, the van or the signage: gmail, outlook,
  hotmail, btconnect, btinternet.
- A copyright year in the footer that is two or more years old, while the
  business is clearly still trading.
- A Facebook or Instagram page more current than the website.
- Several role-based addresses in use publicly (`info@`, `sales@`, `accounts@`,
  `service@`) with no evidence of a shared system behind them.
- Van livery or a fleet photograph implying five to twenty vehicles.
- Membership of a trade body with a public directory listing, especially where
  the listing carries more detail than their own site.
- A "customer portal" link that goes to a third-party product they clearly do not
  control.
- Multiple trading names or brands run by the same directors.

### Tier 3 — timing signals, which make an approach welcome rather than intrusive

- **A new director appointed in the last twelve months**, from Companies House.
  New people arrive with a mandate to change things and a budget to match.
- **A recent move to larger premises, or a second site.** Growth strain is the
  most reliable creator of process pain. Systems that worked at one site fail at
  two.
- **A first-ever job advert for a supervisor, manager or coordinator.** The owner
  has stopped being able to hold it all personally.
- **Accounts filed late, or filed on the final day, more than once.** Modest
  evidence of administrative strain. Note it, do not mention it.
- **A visible price increase or a new service line.** Something is being
  reorganised and the reorganisation needs support.

### The best signal of all

A business owner who describes a task by **how long it takes** rather than by
which software it uses. "That takes Karen most of Thursday" is worth more than
any amount of technology vocabulary. It cannot be spotted from outside, so it is
scored at the first conversation rather than at research, but it is the single
most predictive thing on this page.

## 6. Disqualifiers

These override the score. Any one of them ends the pursuit regardless of how good
everything else looks.

Each hard disqualifier below has a written script in [`saying-no.md`](saying-no.md).
Knowing the boundary is not the same as being able to say it out loud while
somebody is offering money.

### Hard disqualifiers

- **Outside both territories** during phase one.
- **51 or more employees**, or any sign of a procurement process, a preferred
  supplier list or a security questionnaire.
- **An in-house developer or IT manager.** They will, correctly, do this
  themselves, and the engagement becomes an argument about approach.
- **Shopping for "an AI strategy".** If the requirement is framed as a technology
  rather than a problem, the engagement ends badly for both sides. This is the
  clearest disqualifier in the whole document and it is tempting to ignore
  because such enquiries sound exciting and well-funded.
- **Wanting a monthly retainer** and unwilling to consider any other structure.
- **Wanting a technical co-founder, equity payment or deferred payment.**
- **A business that is not viable.** Automating a loss-making process makes the
  loss arrive faster. Signs: a formal insolvency process, a winding-up petition,
  a pattern of charges registered against the company, a dormant filing at
  Companies House while the business appears to be trading.
- **Regulated advice as the deliverable.** Legal, medical or financial advice
  generated or delivered by the system. We build the system, we do not supply the
  advice inside it, and that boundary belongs in the scope document.
- **Franchisees whose systems are dictated by the franchisor.** The decision is
  not theirs and the integration will not be permitted.
- **Multi-site operations whose head office is elsewhere.** The person in the
  room cannot buy.

### Soft disqualifiers — proceed only with eyes open

- **A previous failed technology project inside the last two years.** Not fatal,
  and often the reason they will talk to a smaller supplier. But the first
  conversation has to surface it, and the scope must be smaller than instinct
  suggests. See objection 8 in [`objection-handling.md`](objection-handling.md),
  and section 4.3 of [`saying-no.md`](saying-no.md).
- **Personal or special category data at the centre of the process**, such as
  care records, occupational health, or anything clinical. It is workable, but
  the data protection obligations are real, the scope document has to be right
  and the schema has to be built properly. Do not take this as an early
  engagement while the working practices are still being established.
- **A committee.** More than two people who each need to agree, in a business
  under fifty people, usually means nobody has authority.
- **An enquiry that starts with a finished specification and asks only for a
  price.** Lower value, no diagnosis, and usually being quoted against three
  other suppliers on price alone.

## 7. Scoring

For use in research and, later, in the CRM. Score out of 100. Hard disqualifiers
override the total and set the record to `disqualified` with a reason.

| Dimension | Weight | Scoring |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | 20 | Core 20, edge 10, outside 0 |
| Size | 20 | 5–25 employees 20; 3–4 or 26–50 10; otherwise 0 |
| Sector | 15 | Strong 15, additional-strong 12, neutral 6, weak 0 |
| Observable signals | 30 | Tier 1 signal 10 each, tier 2 signal 3 each, tier 3 signal 5 each, capped at 30 |
| Decision access | 15 | Named director identifiable and contactable 15; named but not contactable 8; no named person 0 |

| Total | Action |
|---|---|
| 70 to 100 | Shortlist for human review |
| 50 to 69 | Hold. Revisit if a timing signal appears. |
| Below 50 | Discard. Record the reason, do not re-add later. |

A score is a filter for research effort, never a substitute for the human
inspection gate in the outreach sequence. The sequence in the master plan has two
human approvals in it and the score does not replace either.

## 8. Sourcing this profile lawfully

The sourcing correction in the master plan applies in full and is repeated here
because this is the file someone will have open while building a list.

**Do not build the core lead database by bulk-exporting Google Maps.** Google's
Maps terms restrict using Maps content to create or augment business listings,
mailing lists or telemarketing lists. Places is pay-as-you-go with field-level
billing rather than a permanent free allowance. Treat Google as one discovery
signal, never the database of record.

**Preferred sources, in rough order of usefulness for this profile:**

1. **Companies House.** Directors, incorporation date, filing history, registered
   address, SIC codes. The source of record for decision access and for the
   timing signals in tier 3.
2. **The businesses' own websites.** Where nearly every tier 1 and tier 2 signal
   is actually found. Also the best material for outreach, because a message can
   refer to something the business said about itself.
3. **Trade body and industry directories.** NICEIC, Gas Safe, FMB, RICS, trade
   association member lists. High precision on sector and often on size.
4. **Local business directories and chambers of commerce.** Nottinghamshire and
   Warwickshire chambers, business improvement districts, town and parish
   business listings.
5. **Council business and licensing registers**, where published.
6. **Job adverts.** The source for several tier 1 signals, and current by
   definition.
7. **Manually verified research** for anything that matters.
8. **Licensed datasets**, once there is money to pay for one. Cheaper and safer
   than extracting more data.

### What the record must carry

Every lead record carries the compliance fields from the master plan, section 5:
`subscriber_type`, `lawful_basis`, `source`, `source_date`,
`privacy_notice_status`, `marketing_status`, `opt_out`, `suppression_list`,
`contact_history`.

`subscriber_type` deserves particular attention for this profile, because the two
territories will produce a mix. ICO guidance distinguishes corporate subscribers
from sole traders and individual subscribers, and the rules for electronic
marketing differ materially between them. Corporate subscribers can generally
receive unsolicited business-to-business electronic marketing without PECR
consent, but identity and opt-out requirements still apply, and personal data
used for business-to-business marketing remains subject to UK data protection
law.

A meaningful share of the trades and rural businesses in both territories will be
sole traders or partnerships rather than limited companies. They are not the same
case as a limited company and must not be filed as one. Determine
`subscriber_type` at the point of adding the record, from the Companies House
result or its absence, and never by guessing from the trading name.

**None of these fields exist in the CRM today.** Until they do, the CRM cannot
lawfully drive outreach. Research and shortlisting can proceed. Sending cannot.

## 9. What we still do not know

Honest gaps. Fill them from real conversations, not from more thinking.

- Whether the Nottingham and Alcester territories behave the same way. They are
  very different: a city with an industrial base against a rural market town
  economy. The profile currently assumes one set of rules for both, and that
  assumption is untested.
- Which of the six categories actually opens doors. The expectation is "save
  time", because it is the easiest to put a number against. "Understand your
  data" may prove easier to sell to the businesses that already know their
  reporting is costing them days, and harder everywhere else.
- The real floor on price. The £800 to £1,500 band in the worked example is a
  reasoned starting point, not a tested one.
- Whether the 5-employee floor is right. It may turn out that well-chosen
  three-person businesses convert better than fifteen-person ones because the
  owner is the decision.

Review this file after the first ten qualified conversations, and rewrite section
5 using the words those businesses actually used.
