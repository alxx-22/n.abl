# Copy deck

Every word currently published on the marketing site, recorded with its source
location. Transcribed from the application on 2026-08-15, after the rewrite to
the six problem-led categories.

**Why this file exists.** The copy lives inside JSX. A wording change is
therefore invisible in review unless someone reads the component. This deck is
the reviewable copy of it, so a change can be argued about in words rather than
in a diff full of markup.

**How to use it.** Change the deck and the source in the same commit. If the two
drift apart, the source is what visitors see and the deck is wrong — fix the
deck. Curly quotes and dashes are written here as they render, not as the JSX
entities.

## 4. What we do — the six categories

`src/pages/Home.jsx:18-31`, `:215-246`

> **Chapter:** What we do
>
> **Heading:** Start with the problem. Not the technology.
>
> **Sub:** Nobody arrives knowing which tool they need. They arrive knowing what
> is annoying them. Find the one that sounds like you.

**01 · Save time** — *The job that eats your week*

> Something gets done by hand, over and over, and it always takes longer than it
> should. We find where the hours go and hand that work to something that does
> not get bored.

**02 · Reduce mistakes** — *The thing that keeps going wrong*

> Retyped numbers, missed steps, the spreadsheet only one person understands. We
> replace the fragile bit with something that does it the same way every time.

**03 · Get more customers** — *The enquiries you never followed up*

> Leads that arrive and go cold, follow-ups that depend on someone remembering.
> We make capture and follow-up happen whether anyone remembers or not.

**04 · Build something new** — *The thing you do not have yet*

> A website, a booking flow, an internal tool, a portal for your customers.
> Scoped properly and quoted at a fixed price before anyone starts.

**05 · Train your team** — *The software you already pay for*

> Most businesses use a fraction of what they own. Sessions built around your
> actual work, not a generic course, so people leave able to do the thing.

**06 · Fix something** — *The bit that broke*

> Something stopped working, or needs changing, or was never quite right. Buy
> credits and spend them when you need us — no monthly retainer to sit on
> standby.

Each body is written in the client's vocabulary, not ours. "The spreadsheet only
one person understands" is the sentence most likely to make somebody recognise
themselves, and it is deliberately unglamorous.

| Figure | Label |
|---|---|

| £40 | A month, once it is built |
| £200 | Saved every month |

The four figures animate up from zero when they scroll into view, and render as
their final value under `prefers-reduced-motion`. This is the same arithmetic as
the master plan, §3. **If the master plan's worked example changes, this changes
with it.**

> **Heading:** Afterwards: credits, not a retainer

| Credit | Copy |

`src/pages/Home.jsx:70-80`, `:389-418`

>

>
> **Sub:** The kind of problem we take on, and the sort of thing that gets built
> to solve it. Examples, not a client list — we name clients only with
> permission, and not before there is something worth naming.

That sub is the most important sentence in this section and must not be cut. It
is what makes the three cards below examples rather than claims.

**Save time — A morning of reporting, down to nothing**
*Power Automate · Excel · Power BI*

> A pack that two people rebuilt by hand every week assembles itself overnight
> and is sitting there before the Monday meeting.

**Reduce mistakes — Orders that stopped being retyped**
*Python · API integration*

> Orders arriving by email were copied into the system by hand, and occasionally
> copied wrong. Now they arrive already in it, and anything unusual is flagged
> rather than guessed at.

**Build something new — A booking page that fills the diary**
*Web · Payments · Calendar sync*

> Enquiries came by phone, always at the worst moment. A booking flow on the site
> takes the ones that never needed a conversation.

No industry line, no client name, no percentage. The previous version of these
cards carried invented attributions ("Retail Operations — UK SME", "Sales Team —
B2B Technology") and invented outcomes ("14 hours of weekly reporting. Gone.",
"60% more accurate"). All of it is deleted.

| "No retainers" | Pricing, credits, footer | True. Core to the model. |

## Rules for changing this copy

1. **British English.** Optimise, not optimize. Organisation, not organization.
2. **Sentence case** everywhere except the nav.
3. **The square dot** is the `<span class="dot" />` element, never a typed full
   stop, and it closes a heading rather than appearing mid-sentence.
4. **No number goes on the page unless it is checkable or labelled
   illustrative.** This is the rule the previous version broke.
5. **No client name without written permission**, and no example dressed up to
   look like a client.
6. **Lead with the problem.** If a new heading names a technology before it names
   a problem, rewrite it.
7. **No superlatives, no "unlock", no "transform your business".** The tone is a
   competent tradesperson quoting a job, not an agency pitching.
8. **The offer copy answers to `01-positioning`.** If the six categories change
   there, they change here — not the other way round.
