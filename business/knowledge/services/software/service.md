# Custom Software

## In one paragraph

A process shaped around a tool that does not fit it, so the answer is a tool
that does not exist yet and has to be built: the internal tool that replaces
the shared spreadsheet nobody may touch, a portal where customers see their
own jobs, certificates or documents, a field app for job sheets and sign-off
on site, a register for stock, assets, plant or documents. We look for the
off-the-shelf answer first and say so if it exists; losing a build and keeping
the client's trust is a good trade. The client says "we need a thing that does
X".

## Signals that point here

Seen on their own pages, in their own words:
- Customers chasing things the business holds: "we will email your
  certificate", "call us for an update on your job", "request a copy of your
  report".
- Field work recorded away from a desk: engineers, installers, surveyors,
  inspectors, cleaners; "our engineer completes a job sheet on site", photos,
  sign-off.
- Certificates, test records or asset registers issued as part of the service:
  gas and electrical safety, fire, lifting, calibration, inspections.
- A multi-stage, trade-specific job described step by step (survey, design,
  approval, install, handover) that a generic product would handle badly.
- A "client login" or "customer portal" link to a third-party product that
  does not fit what they describe.
- Their own words about outgrowing a spreadsheet or a paper system.
- Booking with real constraints a generic tool cannot express: equipment plus
  delivery slots, several resources at once.

## Signals that point somewhere else

- The need is public-facing: a customer booking, buying or enquiring →
  **web**.
- An existing process made faster, with the records already digital →
  **automation**. If they already run a job-management product and the pain is
  rekeying out of it, that is automation too.
- A product for their sector already does it (salon or restaurant booking,
  standard job-management tools) → not ours to build. If they own one and use
  a tenth of it, that is training, which is not scored here.
- The question is what their figures mean → **data_analytics**.
- A signal about the register → no service.

## What we would build

Client portals, and customer-facing document delivery with private storage
and short-lived links instead of attachments; internal tools and staff
dashboards; mobile field apps (job sheets, inspection forms, photo capture,
sign-off); registers for stock, assets, plant, vehicles, tools and documents;
booking and scheduling where the constraints are real. The smallest version
that is genuinely useful comes first.

## What kills it

Visible now, so score low:
- The page already shows a portal or a named industry product doing the job.
- One person, or no sign of staff: nobody to use an internal tool.
- A franchise or branch whose systems are set by head office.
- Register distress: see the ceilings in the scale.

Learned on a call, so use for walk_away_if:
- They cannot say what they do today instead, or what happens on the day it
  does not exist.
- A finished specification and a request for a price only.
- A product to sell to their industry rather than a need of their own,
  especially with equity or payment-when-it-works attached.

## How this specialist scores

Custom software is the biggest commitment we ask a client for and the easiest
to over-sell.
- A sector that "could use an app": 30 at most.
- 60 or more needs an observed workflow that generic tools handle badly, in
  their own words: certificates, site visits, multi-stage jobs, customers
  chasing updates.
- 80 or more needs that, plus evidence the business can carry a build: a team
  or several directors, several years trading, accounts beyond micro-entity.
- Mark down hard when an off-the-shelf product plainly exists for their
  sector, or when the page shows one in use.

## The question that settles it

"What do you do today instead of having this, and who does it?"
