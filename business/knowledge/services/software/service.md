# Custom Software

Grounded in `business/01-positioning/service-categories.md` §4 (Build
something new).

## In one paragraph

A tool that does not exist and has to be built, because nothing off the shelf
fits the way this business works: an internal tool that replaces the shared
spreadsheet nobody is allowed to touch, a client portal, a field app for job
sheets and sign-off on site, a register for stock, assets, plant or documents.
We check for the off-the-shelf answer first and say so if it exists — losing a
build and gaining a client who trusts us is a good trade.

## Signals that point here

- A workflow described on their page that is specific to their trade and
  unlikely to be served by a generic product: inspections with certificates,
  multi-stage jobs, bespoke quoting rules.
- Customers who need to see their own documents, certificates, jobs or history
  ("we will email you your certificate", "call us for an update on your job").
- Field work: engineers, installers, surveyors, cleaners, carers — anyone whose
  record of the job is made away from a desk.
- Regulated record-keeping where a spreadsheet is the current answer and an
  audit trail would matter.
- A business that has grown through several years and several directors while
  still visibly running on email and forms.

## Signals that point somewhere else

- The need is public-facing — booking, buying, enquiring → **web**.
- The same tool exists off the shelf and they simply are not using it →
  not ours to build; say so (training and support, which this pipeline does not
  score).
- An existing process is being made faster rather than a new capability built →
  **automation**.

## What we would build

Client portals; internal tools and staff dashboards; booking and scheduling
where the constraints are real; mobile-friendly field applications (job
sheets, inspection forms, photo capture, sign-off); registers for stock,
assets, plant, vehicles and documents; customer-facing document delivery with
private storage and short-lived links rather than attachments.

## What kills it

- They will not answer "what happens on the day it does not exist?"
- A finished specification and a request for a price only.
- The build is a product venture to sell to their industry rather than a need
  of their own business.

## How this specialist scores

Custom software is the most expensive thing we sell and the easiest to
over-sell. A sector that "could use an app" is 30 at most. 60 or more needs an
observed workflow that generic tools handle badly — their own words describing
certificates, site visits, multi-step jobs or customers chasing updates. 80 or
more needs that plus evidence the business is large enough to pay for a build:
several directors or staff, several years trading, accounts beyond micro.

Mark down when the page already shows a portal or a named industry product
doing the job.

## The question that settles it

"What do you do today instead of having this, and who does it?"
