# The scale, and what agreement means

Every score is a whole number from 0 to 100, for **one service at one
business**. It answers one question:

> How strong is the case for writing to this business about this service?

It is not a probability of a sale, and it is not a judgement of the business.

| Score | What has to be true |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Their own page shows the problem this service fixes, in their own words, and the business is the size and kind we sell to. A quote carries it. |
| 60–79 | Something observed — a quote from their page, or a register fact that could only be read one way — makes the need likely, and nothing argues against it. |
| 40–59 | Plausible and worth one question on a call. The sector and the register suggest it; nothing on their page shows it. |
| 20–39 | Only the sector suggests it, or something on the page argues against it. Not worth a letter on its own. |
| 0–19 | No case. The honest answer is "not this service". |

## The rules every agent is held to in code

- **A score must cite signals.** A proposal that names no signal, or names one
  that was not agreed in the research review, is refused and does not count as
  a proposal.
- **A score is only decided by agreement.** The sales agent and the specialist
  each propose a number. It is settled when one of them accepts the other's
  last number exactly. "Close enough" is not agreement; say the number.
- **No agreement, no score.** If the rounds run out, the service is recorded as
  disputed and the lead gets no number for it. A person reads the argument.
- **A specialist may hand a lead on.** If the signals are really about another
  service, say which and why; the sales agent decides whether to bring that
  specialist in.
- **Nobody invents a contact route.** No email address, phone number, web
  address or postcode appears in anything an agent says. A message that carries
  one is refused whole.

## Arguing well

Say what you think and why, in plain sentences, to the agent you are talking
to. Point at signals by their id. If the other agent is right, say so and take
their number — agreeing quickly with a good argument is the job, not a defeat.
If they are wrong, say which signal they have misread.
