# The scale, and what agreement means

Every score is a whole number from 0 to 100, for **one service at one
business**. It answers one question: how strong is the case for writing to
this business about this service? It is not a probability of a sale and not a
judgement of the business. It is a hypothesis about where a first call would
start; most of what kills a deal is only learned on that call.

**Only signals that point here count.** A service may be argued only on
signals that match its own "Signals that point here". A signal built on the
register alone (age, accounts, directors, an overdue filing, charges) points
at no service. If the signals you were brought are not about your service,
pass or redirect. Do not score on them.

| Score | What has to be true |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Their own words show the problem this service fixes; it plainly repeats (volume, every job, many customers); the business is our size (a team or several sites on the page, small-company accounts, several directors); nothing in "What kills it" is visible. |
| 60–79 | At least one fact quoted from their own page matches a "Signals that point here" line, and nothing visible argues against it. |
| 40–59 | Plausible. An observed fact points here only indirectly, or size and budget are unclear. Worth one question on a call, not yet a letter. |
| 20–39 | Only the kind of business suggests it (sector alone is 30 at most), or a fact points here but something visible argues against it. |
| 1–19 | Nothing observed points here. |
| 0 | No fit. Pass. |

**Ceilings, which override the table**
- Dormant accounts, insolvency history, or the business is itself an IT,
  software or web firm: 0 for every service.
- A filing overdue: the CAUTION line sets the ceiling, and code enforces it.
- Incorporated in the last year or so with nothing showing trade; one
  director, micro-entity accounts and no sign of staff: 35 at most. Say which
  one applies.
- No website found and nothing else pointing here: 20 at most. Not finding a
  site is unknown, not a need.
- Evidence resting on sector, a menu item or other page furniture: 30 at most.
- Weak-fit sectors (salons, restaurants, pubs, takeaways, walk-in retail): 30
  at most unless their page shows a gap the usual software leaves.

## The rules every agent is held to in code

- **A score must cite signals.** A proposal that names no signal, or one not
  agreed in the research review, is refused and does not count.
- **Sector is not a signal.** A signal true of nearly every business of its
  kind (electricians issue test certificates, engineers work on site) is
  marked as the sector's. A service argued only on such signals is read at
  30 at most, whatever number is named. The first Notts run scored two
  electricians 60 and 70 on exactly that.
- **A score is only decided by agreement.** Sales and the specialist each
  propose a number. It is settled when one accepts the other's last number
  exactly. "Close enough" is not agreement; say the number.
- **No agreement, no score.** If the rounds run out, the service is recorded
  as disputed and a person reads the argument.
- **A specialist may hand a lead on.** If the signals are really about another
  service, say which and why; sales decides whether to bring it in.
- **Nobody invents a contact route.** No email address, phone number, web
  address or postcode in anything an agent says. A message carrying one is
  refused whole.

## Arguing well

- Open by naming the signal ids that put the lead in your band, and the one
  piece of evidence that would move it up a band. No such signal, no score
  above 40.
- Never go up a band because the other agent sounds sure. Move only when they
  point to a signal you misread or missed. "Could", "plausible" and "many
  businesses like this" are not evidence.
- Do not split the difference. If you accept, say which signal makes their
  number right. If not, say which signal they have over-read.
- Say what is missing. "Nobody read their booking page" is a reason to stay
  low, not to guess.
- Agreeing quickly with a good argument is the job. Agreeing with a number
  nobody has evidenced is not.
