# Data & Analytics

Grounded in `business/01-positioning/service-categories.md` §3 (Understand your
data).

## In one paragraph

They hold information they cannot answer questions with. Almost never a "big
data" problem: the numbers exist, they live in three or four places that
disagree, and assembling them by hand costs somebody a day a month — so it
happens late or not at all, and decisions get made on memory. The work is
getting it into one place, making it trustworthy, and making it answer the
question somebody actually asks. Start with the decision, not the data.

## Signals that point here

- Several places the same facts live, visible from outside: an online shop and
  a shop floor, several branches or sites, an accounts package and a separate
  booking system.
- A business whose margins depend on knowing which jobs pay: contractors,
  trades with many small jobs, wholesalers, manufacturers, agencies billing
  time.
- Stock, fleet, plant or assets mentioned in volume.
- Several years of trading with a growing team or several sites — the point at
  which "I know roughly how we are doing" stops being true.
- Accounts filed as small rather than micro: enough activity for the numbers to
  matter and to be spread across systems.

## Signals that point somewhere else

- The numbers are fine and one person retypes them between two systems →
  **automation**.
- They want customers to see their own records → **web** or **software**.
- The underlying records do not exist yet; the first job is capturing them →
  **software** or **automation**, and say so.

## What we would build

Data cleaning and reconciliation so a customer, product or job means the same
thing everywhere; one dataset assembled on a schedule from the systems already
in use; reporting that builds itself overnight; dashboards only where a live
view changes a decision — otherwise a scheduled email with five numbers;
job-level profitability and utilisation; spreadsheets done properly, which is
often the right answer.

## What kills it

- The underlying data does not exist, or nobody will own its accuracy.
- They want a dashboard because a competitor has one — no decision attached.
- The answer already sits in a report they own and do not open.

## How this specialist scores

The page rarely shows this problem directly, so be honest about that: most
leads here are a 40–59 on sector, size and structure. Go to 60 or more only when
something observed shows the data is split or the decisions depend on it —
several branches or channels named on their own page, stock or fleet in
volume, a register fact that shows real scale. 80 or more needs their own words
about reporting, figures, visibility or "knowing where we are".

A business too small to have more than one system is under 30: there is nothing
to join up.

## The question that settles it

"What decision would you make differently next month if you had a better answer
to it — and where do the numbers for it live now?"
