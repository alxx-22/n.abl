# Data & Analytics

## In one paragraph

A question they plainly need answered that their records cannot answer. Almost
never "big data": the numbers exist in three or four places that disagree,
assembling them by hand costs somebody a day or two a month, so it happens
late or not at all and decisions get made on memory. We get the numbers into
one place, make them trustworthy, and make them answer the question somebody
actually asks: a report that builds itself, a spreadsheet done properly, a
dashboard only where a live view changes a decision. Start with the decision,
not the data.

## Signals that point here

Seen on their own pages, in their own words:
- The same facts in several places: an online shop and a trade counter;
  several branches, depots or sites; several brands run together.
- Stock, fleet, plant or hire equipment in volume: a large product range, a
  hire catalogue, a fleet of vans.
- Many jobs or orders where margin by job matters, stated on the page:
  contractors, installers, fabricators, wholesalers, firms billing time.
- Their own words about their figures: reporting, KPIs, "knowing where we
  are", visibility across sites.
- A recent second site or move to larger premises: systems that worked at one
  site fail at two.

## Signals that point somewhere else

- The numbers are fine and a person retypes them between systems →
  **automation**.
- A report the business sends its own customers (landlord statements, monthly
  client packs) built by hand → **automation**. That is document building,
  not analysis.
- Customers need to see their own records → **software**.
- The records do not exist yet, or job sheets are still on paper →
  **software** or **automation** first. Say so.
- A signal about the register → no service. Good accounts are budget, not
  need.

## What we would build

Cleaning and reconciliation so a customer, product or job means the same
thing everywhere; one dataset assembled on a schedule from the systems already
in use; the monthly pack that builds itself overnight; job-level
profitability, utilisation, where the time actually goes; a scheduled email
with five numbers where a dashboard would be decoration; spreadsheets done
properly, which is often the right answer.

## What kills it

Visible now, so score low:
- Too small to have more than one system: one site, one till, one person.
  There is nothing to join.
- The page shows a reporting or management system already doing the job.
- Register distress: see the ceilings in the scale.

Learned on a call, so use for walk_away_if:
- The underlying data does not exist, or nobody will own its accuracy.
- A dashboard wanted because a competitor has one, with no decision attached.
- The answer is already in a report they own and do not open.

## How this specialist scores

This problem rarely shows on a website, and owners rarely raise it unprompted
because they have stopped believing the numbers are gettable. Be honest about
that, and do not fill the gap with the sector.
- Sector alone, even with healthy accounts: 30 at most. Size is not need.
- An observed fact suggesting more than one system (a shop and a counter, two
  sites) but nothing about their figures: 40–55.
- 60 or more needs the split observed on their page (several sites, channels
  or brands; stock, fleet or plant in volume; many jobs where margin matters)
  and a business big enough for it to matter: a team, small-company accounts
  or bigger, several directors.
- 80 or more needs their own words about reporting, figures or visibility on
  top of that.
- A micro-entity or one-site business with no sign of more than one system:
  under 30.

## The question that settles it

"What decision would you make differently next month if you had a better
answer to it — and where do the numbers for it live now?"
