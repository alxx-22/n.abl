# The quote template

One page. One number. Written from a filled-in worksheet, never from a
conversation you are recalling.

**Status: never sent.** No quote has been issued and `Q-2026-001` does not exist.
This is a draft.

---

## What this replaces, and why

The old quote template was a list of Power Platform line items: an app, a set of
flows, connectors, licence counts, environment setup, each with its own price,
totalled at the bottom. It is retired. Delete it wherever it is found, along with
anything else built on the same shape.

Four reasons it was wrong, in order of weight:

1. **It sold tool assembly.** A priced list of components says the product is the
   components. n.abl is a technology implementation partner: the product is the
   problem going away, and the tools chosen are our business, not a menu the
   client orders from.
2. **It named one vendor's stack in the quote.** The whole repositioning is that
   the tool follows the problem. A template that assumes Power Platform decides
   the answer before the diagnosis, and it dates badly the moment the right
   answer is a Python script, a static page or a change to something they already
   own.
3. **It invited line-by-line negotiation.** Priced parts get argued about
   individually, dropped to fit a budget, and reassembled into something that
   does not work. The number is for the outcome, and the outcome is not divisible.
4. **It leaked hours.** A component list with prices beside it is one division
   away from an implied hourly rate, and the first question it provokes is "what
   is your day rate?". There is no day rate. Hourly pricing punishes us for
   getting faster, and getting faster is the entire point of how the work is
   built.

What replaces it is a page with the problem, the arithmetic, the price, what is
included, what is not, and how it is paid for.

---

## Rules for every quote

- **One page.** If it does not fit, the scope is too big or the writing is loose.
  Detail lives in the scope of work, not here.
- **One number.** A single fixed price. Not a range, not a from-price, not
  "starting at". Ranges appear in this folder to describe the method; they never
  appear on a document going to a client.
- **One pricing category.** Category A or category B, never both on one page. A
  job that genuinely spans both is two quotes or one scope, decided before
  writing.
- **Numbered, and traceable.** `Q-YYYY-NNN`, beginning `Q-2026-001`. The
  scope-of-work template in `04-legal` expects that reference. The filled-in
  worksheet is stored beside it, so every figure can be traced to something
  somebody said.
- **Paired with a scope of work.** The quote states the price. The scope of work
  states what is being bought and what acceptance means. They are signed together
  and they must not contradict each other.
- **Expected, not guaranteed.** Every projected figure is described as expected
  and attributed to the client's own numbers.
- **British English, plain, no adjectives doing the work of numbers.**

## Template B — capability solution

Something the business did not previously have: a portal, an app, a website, an
internal tool.

**There is no ROI section, and inventing one is the single most likely mistake.**
There is no before-figure, because the process did not exist. A projected return
on something nobody has ever done is a number nobody believes, including us. The
price is fixed, the scope is defined, and changes go through credits or a new
quote.

Sections 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 are as template A. Sections 2, 3 and 4 are
replaced by these two:

---

**2. What you want to be able to do**

`[The capability, in their words. What they cannot do today, who needs to be able
to do it, and what it changes about how the business runs. This is the
description that replaces the arithmetic, so it has to carry its weight.]`

---

**3. The price**

| | |
|---|---|
| **`[Name of the thing]`** | **£`[N]`, fixed** |

This is a fixed price for the scope described. It does not change if the work
takes longer than expected — that is our risk, not yours. It changes only if you
change what you want, and we agree that in writing before the work is done, not
after.

`[Do not add a projected return here. If a genuine measurable saving exists
alongside the new capability, the job has a category A component and should be
scoped as such, on its own quote.]`

## Before you send

- [ ] Every guidance line in square brackets deleted.
- [ ] Every `[PLACEHOLDER]` filled or the section removed.
- [ ] One pricing category. One price. One page.
- [ ] The arithmetic adds up, and it adds up in front of somebody with a
      calculator.
- [ ] Every figure in section 2 came from the client, and the worksheet says
      which call it came from.
- [ ] The residual is stated and is not zero.
- [ ] Section 6 has at least three real exclusions.
- [ ] No hourly rate, no day rate, no hours, no internal cost, anywhere.
- [ ] No tool or vendor names used as line items.
- [ ] No guaranteed saving. "Expected" and "projection" appear where they should.
- [ ] No invented statistics, client names, case studies or testimonials.
- [ ] No claim that any document has been reviewed by a solicitor. None have.
- [ ] Credits section has real numbers or has been deleted.
- [ ] A matching scope of work exists and references the same quote number.
- [ ] The quote number is recorded with its worksheet.
- [ ] Read it once as the client. Anything you would query, they will query.

## Answers to the two questions this document provokes

**"Can you break the price down?"**

> "It is one price for the outcome, and it does not move if the work turns out to
> be harder than I thought. What I can break down is what you get and what
> happens when — that is the scope of work, and it is as detailed as you want it.
> What I do not do is price it by the hour, because then my estimate becomes your
> risk."

**"What is your day rate?"**

> "There isn't one. I price the result, not the time. If I find a faster way to
> build this next month, that is my problem to solve and my benefit to keep — it
> does not change what this costs you."

Do not soften either answer. The fixed number is the strongest part of the
commercial model. See `01-positioning/objection-handling.md`, objections 4 and 5.
