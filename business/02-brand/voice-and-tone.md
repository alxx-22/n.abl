# Voice and tone

Last substantive revision: 2026-08-15.

How n.abl writes. This applies to the website, emails, proposals, the portal, the
team space, documents given to clients, and anything else with the name on it.

The visual identity is in [`brand-guidelines.md`](brand-guidelines.md). What we
say, as opposed to how we say it, is in `business/01-positioning/`.

---

## 1. The voice in one paragraph

Plain, direct and confident. We explain what a thing does, what it costs and what
it will not do. We use numbers where we have them and say so where we do not. We
do not sell with adjectives, because the people we want to work with have been
sold to with adjectives before and it did not go well for them.

The test: read it aloud. If it sounds like a competent person explaining
something to another competent person, it is right. If it sounds like a landing
page, it is wrong.

## 2. The one sentence everything hangs off

> n.abl is a technology implementation partner for small businesses. We find the
> expensive, repetitive or fragile part of a business and build the most
> appropriate fix.

And the sentence that stops it drifting:

> AI is one tool among several, alongside Python, web development, integration,
> automation and training. If the best answer is not AI, we do not force AI into
> it.

**n.abl is not an AI automation agency.** That phrase does not appear in any
document we write. Neither does "AI-first", "AI-powered", "AI-driven" or "AI
transformation" as a description of the business.

The offer is organised by the client's problem, not our toolbox. The customer
question is *what are you trying to improve?* and the answers are the six
categories: save time, reduce mistakes, get more customers, build something new,
train your team, fix something.

Innovation / Automation / Optimisation may still appear as a strapline. It must
never be the structure of a page, a pitch or a proposal.

## 3. Rules

**British English.** Optimisation, organise, specialise, recognise, licence as
the noun and license as the verb, programme for a scheme and program for
software. Full stops outside quotation marks unless the quoted text includes it.

**Short paragraphs.** Two to four sentences. A paragraph that runs eight lines on
a phone will not be read.

**Short sentences.** If a sentence has three clauses, it is probably two
sentences.

**Concrete over abstract.** "It takes twelve hours a month" beats "significant
manual effort". "£1,200" beats "an affordable investment".

**No superlatives.** Not world-class, cutting-edge, best-in-class, revolutionary,
seamless, game-changing, next-generation, bespoke, or leverage as a verb.

**No filler.** Nothing unlocks anyone's potential. Nothing takes a business to the
next level. Nobody is on a journey.

**No em-dash-heavy prose.** One per document at most, and usually none. A comma,
a full stop or a colon does the job with less noise.

**Say the limit.** Every honest description of what we do includes what it does
not do. That is the part that builds trust, and it is the part most copy leaves
out.

**Second person for the client, first person plural for us.** "You send us the
spreadsheet, we build the importer." Not "clients are onboarded".

**Active voice.** "We removed the manual step", not "the manual step was
removed".

## 4. Words we use, and words we do not

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| AI automation agency | technology implementation partner |
| solution (standalone) | the thing it actually is: a script, an importer, a portal, a form |
| leverage | use |
| utilise | use |
| bespoke | built for you, or nothing at all |
| seamless | it works, or say what it connects to |
| cutting-edge | nothing. Delete the sentence. |
| synergy, holistic, ecosystem | nothing. Delete the sentence. |
| investment (meaning price) | price, or cost |
| onboarding journey | what happens first |
| reach out | email us, call us |
| circle back | follow up |
| we're excited to | delete, then state the fact |
| retainer, monthly plan | credits, bought when you need them |
| AI-powered, AI-driven | say what the software does |
| 10x, transform, revolutionise | the actual number, or nothing |

Words that are fine and should be used more: cost, hours, price, fix, build,
own, remove, check, block, error, wrong, no.

## 5. Naming

**n.abl** is always lowercase, always with the full stop, in running text and in
headings. Not N.abl, not NABL, not Nabl, not n.abl® and not "the n.abl team" if
"we" will do.

At the end of a sentence, do not add a second full stop directly after the name.
Rewrite so the name is not the last word.

The **square dot** may replace the full stop on a key headline, and it is the
element that does that job. Do not use it as bullet decoration everywhere, or it
stops meaning anything.

Product and offer names, capitalised as shown:

- **n.abl credits**, the operating layer. Three redemption types: **Build**,
  **Assist**, **Educate**.
- **Efficiency solutions** and **capability solutions** are internal pricing
  categories. Clients hear "we price this on what it saves you" and "this one is
  a fixed price", not the category name.

Money: `£1,200`, `£2,400 a year`, `£20/hour`. Use "a year" and "a month" in
prose, not "p.a." or "/mo". Round in copy, be exact in a quote.

Dates in running text: 15 August 2026. In filenames and tables: 2026-08-15.

Time saved is in hours and minutes, never in percentages alone. "Twelve hours a
month down to two" tells someone something. "An 83% reduction" does not.

## 6. Numbers and the worked example

Every claim about money is a claim someone can check in front of us, or it is not
made.

This is the worked example. Reuse it. Do not invent a different one with better
numbers.

> A task takes 12 hours a month. At a £20/hour loaded cost, that is **£240 a
> month**.
>
> After implementation it takes 2 hours a month, so **£40 a month**.
>
> That is **£200 a month saved**, or **£2,400 a year**.
>
> An implementation priced at roughly **£800 to £1,500** lets the client see it
> plainly: spend about £1,200 once, remove about £2,400 a year of labour.

Two things about how that reads. The arithmetic is deliberately simple enough to
follow without a calculator. And it ends at £2,400 rather than compounding it
over five years, because the five-year figure is the one nobody believes.

Internally we track our own labour cost on every job. We do not sell it. Hourly
pricing punishes us for getting faster, and getting faster is the whole point of
the tooling. Never quote an hourly rate in client-facing copy.

## 7. Things that must be said correctly

Getting any of these wrong in front of a client is worse than saying nothing.

**Claude Code is not a local Claude model.** Claude Code is a local interface and
orchestration environment. The Claude models themselves are cloud-hosted. Running
the tool on our own machines does not move the model onto our own machines. The
saving comes from routing: sending low-intelligence work to ordinary code or a
genuinely local open model, and spending Claude only where intelligence is worth
paying for. Anyone who describes it the other way round will eventually say it
out loud to a client.

**There are no retainers.** Not "flexible retainers", not "a light monthly
plan". The line is: *"You do not pay us monthly to be on standby. You buy support
when you actually need support."*

**Clients own what is built.** Say it plainly and early.

**Google Maps is not our lead database.** Google's Maps terms restrict using Maps
content to create or augment business listings, mailing lists or telemarketing
lists. It is one discovery signal, never the database of record.

**Marketing compliance is a database feature, not a promise.** When describing
how we handle contact data, describe the mechanism: opt-outs are blocked at the
send path, not filtered by someone remembering to filter them. Note that ICO
guidance treats corporate subscribers differently from sole traders and
individual subscribers, and do not flatten that distinction into "B2B email is
fine".

**No legal document has been reviewed by a solicitor.** The privacy notice, the
terms and the cookie page were written in-house. Never state or imply otherwise,
in any document, on the site, or in conversation. If asked, say so directly.

## 8. What we never invent

Not once, not as a placeholder that might survive to production, not "just to
show the layout".

- Client names, logos and testimonials.
- Case studies, including anonymised ones describing work not done.
- Statistics, percentages, industry figures and "studies show".
- Team size, years trading, number of projects delivered.
- Certifications, partnerships and accreditations.
- Awards.

We have no client work to point to yet. The honest answer to "who else have you
worked with?" is in `business/01-positioning/objection-handling.md`, and it does
not involve inventing anyone.

Where a real value is not yet known, write `[PLACEHOLDER]` with what it is
waiting on, like this:

```
Credit pack prices: [PLACEHOLDER — set alongside the first three real quotes]
```

A visible placeholder is a task. An invented number is a liability, and it will
be quoted back to us.

## 9. Formatting

- Sentence case for headings. Not Title Case, not ALL CAPS in body copy.
- Uppercase is permitted only for small eyebrow labels, tracked at
  `--track-wide`, and never for more than four words.
- Bold for the load-bearing phrase in a paragraph, sparingly. Italics almost
  never.
- Lists for things that are genuinely a list. Prose for things that are an
  argument.
- Tables for anything with two or more columns of real data. Do not build a table
  out of prose.
- Code font for identifiers, file paths, access keys and figures that need to
  line up. Not for emphasis.
- One idea per link. Link text says where it goes. Never "click here".
- Every image needs alt text that says what it shows, not what it is called.

## 10. Rewrites

The same content, wrong and then right.

**Website hero**

> Wrong: n.abl is a cutting-edge AI automation agency helping ambitious
> businesses unlock their potential through seamless, bespoke AI solutions.

> Right: Tell us what's costing you time, money or accuracy. We build the fix.

**Service description**

> Wrong: Our Automation pillar leverages best-in-class AI to transform your
> operational workflows and drive exponential efficiency gains.

> Right: If a job is done by hand every week and always the same way, it can
> usually be done by software instead. We find those jobs, price the fix against
> what the job currently costs you, and build it.

**Pricing**

> Wrong: Investment starts from just £800 for a tailored efficiency solution
> designed around your unique needs.

> Right: A task taking 12 hours a month at £20/hour costs you £240 a month. Cut
> it to 2 hours and you save £2,400 a year. We would price that implementation
> at £800 to £1,500, once, and you own it.

**Support**

> Wrong: Our flexible support retainers ensure you're never alone on your
> automation journey.

> Right: You do not pay us monthly to be on standby. You buy credits and spend
> them when something needs building, fixing or explaining.

**Capability, honestly**

> Wrong: Trusted by businesses across the Midlands.

> Right: n.abl is new. We have no client work to show yet, so the first
> conversation is a free look at one process, and you keep the analysis either
> way.
