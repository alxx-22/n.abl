# Brand promise and the three pillars

The verbal half of the identity. `brand-guidelines.md` covers how n.abl looks;
this file covers what it says about itself and what it is remembered by.

Recorded 2026-08-16, after the pillars were restored to brand level.

---

## 1. The promise

> **We make your business work smarter.**

This is the headline. It leads the homepage, and it is the sentence somebody
repeats to a friend.

**"Technology implementation partner for small businesses" is the descriptor,
not the promise.** It is accurate, it is what goes in the eyebrow above the
headline, in a directory listing and in the Companies House description — and
nobody has ever remembered it. Both are true at once, and the order matters:
promise first, category underneath.

## 2. The three pillars

> **INNOVATION** — Find better ways to do things.
> **AUTOMATION** — Take repetitive work off people's hands.
> **OPTIMISATION** — Get more from the people, processes, systems and data you
> already have.
>
> In practice that means: Automation · Data & Analytics · Software · Web · AI ·
> Training.

Set as a mnemonic, the terms carry a middle dot and no ampersand:

> Innovation · Automation · Optimisation

### What the pillars are for

They are **brand architecture**. Their job is memorability. They belong in:

- the strapline
- the band beneath the homepage hero
- the footer
- a business card, a deck cover, an email signature
- the sentence you say when someone asks what n.abl does and you have four
  seconds

### What the pillars are not for

They are **not the offer structure**. They must never be:

- the site navigation
- a set of service pages
- the contents page of a proposal
- the way a call is opened
- something a client is asked to choose between

That job belongs to the six customer-problem categories in
`01-positioning/service-categories.md`. A client with a job that takes twelve
hours a month does not know which pillar that is, and asking them to guess makes
them feel stupid at the moment we most need them talking freely.

### The history, so it does not repeat

The pillars were originally the offer structure, which was wrong. The
repositioning in `7db234d` fixed that by **deleting them entirely**, along with
"We make your business work smarter", which was an overcorrection: it removed
the only two memorable things the business had in order to solve a problem that
was about placement, not wording.

Both are now restored at brand level, and the offer stays problem-led. If you
find a document that says the pillars survive "only as a strapline, if they
survive at all", that document predates this one and the hedge is what caused
the deletion.

## 3. Voice

Curious, practical, technically capable, straight-talking, non-pushy,
economically aware.

The distinction that matters:

> **Not** *"We know the answer."*
> **But** *"We know how to help you find the answer."*

That is the correct register for a consultancy without years of case studies,
and it is also simply more honest.

Full guidance in [`voice-and-tone.md`](voice-and-tone.md). The rules in short:

- **British English throughout.** Optimisation, prioritise, recognise, analyse,
  licence as the noun.
- Short sentences. Concrete nouns.
- Never imply scale, client count or track record the business does not have.
- Never open with the technology.
- Second person for the client, first person plural for us. Active voice.

## 4. Banned language

The full substitution table is in `voice-and-tone.md` §4. Two categories matter
enough to restate here.

### 4.1 Marketing filler

`leverage` · `utilise` · `solutions-focused` · `cutting-edge` · `seamless` ·
`bespoke` · `synergy` · `holistic` · `ecosystem` · `AI-powered` · `AI-driven` ·
`10x` · `transform` · `revolutionise` · `reach out` · `circle back` ·
`we're excited to` · `AI automation agency`

### 4.2 Judgemental and gatekeeping framing

**The rule:** n.abl never decides whether a customer's problem is important
enough to solve *before hearing it*. The emotional experience must be **"tell us
everything, we're listening"**, and expertise comes after.

| Never write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "Let's establish what is actually worth fixing" | "Tell us what's getting in the way" |
| "We'll tell you if it's worth it" | "We'll show you the arithmetic and you can decide" |
| "Is this problem big enough for us?" | "Tell us what isn't working" |
| "Qualifying your requirement" | "Understanding how the work happens today" |
| Any phrasing that grades the problem before hearing it | "Tell us what's taking longer than it should" |

**What this rule does not ban.** n.abl still declines work that would not pay for
itself, and still says so. That is the most trust-building thing the business
does and it is load-bearing in the pricing model. The difference is *when* and
*how*:

- **Banned** — grading the problem as a precondition of the conversation.
- **Kept** — after listening, showing the arithmetic, and leaving the decision
  with the customer. "Here's what this costs you and what it would cost to fix.
  On these numbers it takes eighteen months to pay back — that's your call, not
  mine."

The first is a gate. The second is advice, with the working shown. Only the
first is a problem.
