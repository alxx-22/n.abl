# Pub: every use case

What callers ring a UK pub about, what a great receptionist does, and what
the demo needs. Researched on 13 October 2026 (UK rules checked against
current sources), then checked for gaps by the same session, not a second
reviewer (see "Checked for gaps" at the end). 43 use cases: 9 Must, 19
Should, 15 Could.

**The sample business**: The Weaver's Arms, 9 Lace Row (example), Nottingham
NG1, so the pub shares a city with the café, the barber and the takeaway (a
default; see Decisions for Alex).
- **Hours**: Monday to Thursday 12pm to 11pm, Friday and Saturday 12pm to
  midnight, Sunday 12pm to 10:30pm.
- **Food**: Monday to Saturday 12pm to 9pm. Sunday roasts from 12pm to 6pm.
- **Rooms**:
  - **The bar**: a few tables that can be booked, the rest first come. The
    big screens are here.
  - **The dining room**: every table can be booked.
  - **The beer garden**: first come, weather permitting. Smoking allowed.
  - **The Loft**: the room upstairs, for up to 40. Hire by enquiry.
- **Children**: welcome with an adult until 9pm.
- **Dogs**: welcome everywhere (Alex, 13 October).
- **What's on**:
  - the quiz on Thursdays at 8pm (teams of up to six, £2 a head);
  - live music on Saturdays from 9pm;
  - sport on the big screens, from the manager's list of fixtures.
- **Drinks**: four cask ales that change, lagers and ciders, wine by the
  125ml, 175ml or 250ml glass, a gluten-free beer in bottles, and
  alcohol-free beer.
- **Money**: card and cash, with no charge for card. Tips go to the team.

## Who calls

- People booking a table for dinner or Sunday lunch, often with children or
  a dog
- People asking if they can just turn up, sit in the garden, or watch the
  match
- People asking what's on: the match, the quiz, live music
- Teams booking the quiz
- People planning a party, a wake, a christening or a work do in the room
  upstairs
- People asking what's on tap, about gluten-free beer or alcohol-free drinks
- People asking about allergies, vegan food and whether the kitchen is still
  serving
- Parents asking whether children can come, and whether a 16 or 17-year-old
  can have a drink with a meal
- People asking about ID, dogs, parking, access, smoking and taxis
- Customers who left something behind, are unhappy, or felt unwell after
  eating
- Neighbours complaining about noise
- Staff ringing in sick, suppliers, brewery deliveries, job applicants,
  sales calls

## The moments that sell it in a demo

1. "Are you showing the Forest game on Saturday?": yes, from the manager's
   list of fixtures, with the kick-off time and where the screens are.
   Then the honest answer on tables: the bar's aren't booked for big
   matches, so come early; the dining room can be booked, but it has no
   screen.
2. "Sunday lunch for six at 1, two kids and our dog": all welcome (dogs
   everywhere, children before 9pm), a real table, the dog noted for the
   team, booked and texted. At 8:30pm with the children, the honest answer:
   children are welcome until 9pm, so an earlier table.
3. "Can my 17-year-old have a pint with his dinner?": the law said plainly.
   With a table meal, a 16 or 17-year-old may have beer, wine or cider
   that an adult buys; never at the bar, and staff will ask for ID.
4. "What ales are on?": today's list, set by the manager on the back
   office's "Today"; the prospect changes it and the next caller hears the
   change.
5. "Can I book a team for the quiz on Thursday? Five of us": booked, the
   £2 a head said, starting at 8pm.
6. "Can we hire the room upstairs for a 40th in March?": a message for the
   manager with the date, how many and what they have in mind. No price is
   promised.
7. The prospect marks the roast beef sold out on "Today": a caller asking
   hears it's gone, and what's left (the pork or the nut roast).

## Use cases

### Tables and turning up

#### Book a table for food (Must)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:**
- A real table from the booking tools, in the bar or the dining room, at a
  time the kitchen is serving.
- Read back, booked, texted.
- Allergies and dietary needs asked once, and noted for the kitchen.

#### Can we just turn up? (Must)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:**
- The garden and most of the bar are first come.
- A table can be booked for food. The busy times are said honestly, and a
  first-come table is never promised.

#### Sunday lunch (Must)

**Caller:** Families, groups

**The receptionist:**
- Roasts from 12pm to 6pm, with tables held for two hours. Sunday is the
  busiest day, so booking is recommended.
- If a roast has sold out today, it's said, with what's left.

#### Change or cancel a booking (Must)

**Caller:** Someone with a booking

**The receptionist:** Found by the reference or the calling number, changed
or cancelled, and texted. Without a deposit, nothing is kept.

#### Running late (Should)

**Caller:** Someone with a booking

**The receptionist:** Noted on the booking. The table is held for the time
the manager sets (15 minutes), and the caller is told how long.

#### A big group (Should)

**Caller:** Someone organising

**The receptionist:**
- Up to eight at a table. More than that is a message for the manager,
  who can join tables or offer the room upstairs.
- Nothing is promised.

#### With children (Must)

**Caller:** Parents

**The receptionist:**
- Children are welcome with an adult until 9pm.
- A booking with children ends by then; later, a table is for over-18s.
- High chairs and the children's menu, from the manager's answers.

**Rules:**
- Children under 16 must be with an adult in premises used mainly for
  alcohol, and may not be there unaccompanied between midnight and 5am
  (Licensing Act 2003 s145:
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/145).
- The 9pm limit is the pub's own policy, from its licence, said as the
  pub's policy, never as the law.

#### With a dog (Should)

**Caller:** Dog owners

**The receptionist:**
- Dogs are welcome everywhere (Alex, 13 October), noted on the booking so
  there's a water bowl.
- Assistance dogs are welcome whatever the dog rule says, should the
  manager change it.

**Rules:** Refusing an assistance dog is disability discrimination
(Equality Act 2010).

#### Wheelchair access (Should)

**Caller:** Customer or carer

**The receptionist:** The manager's access answer (step-free, an accessible
toilet), and a step-free table on the booking.

#### The beer garden and the weather (Could)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** First come, weather permitting. No garden table is
booked or promised.

### What's on

#### Are you showing the match? (Must)

**Caller:** Sports fans

**The receptionist:**
- Only fixtures on the manager's list are said, with their kick-off time
  and where they're shown (the bar's big screens). "We'll probably have it
  on" is never said.
- Bar tables aren't booked for big matches; that is the manager's rule,
  said honestly.

**Rules:** Showing a match needs the broadcaster's commercial subscription.
That's the pub's business, never the receptionist's to discuss.

#### Book a team for the quiz (Should)

**Caller:** Quiz teams

**The receptionist:**
- A team booking on Thursday at 8pm, up to six, with the team name.
- The £2 a head entry is said.

#### Live music and events this week (Should)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** What's on from the manager's list: the band, the
time, whether it's free.

**Rules:** Amplified live music between 8am and 11pm, to an audience of up
to 500, needs no licence in a pub (Live Music Act 2012:
https://www.cheshirewestandchester.gov.uk/business/licensing-and-permits/entertainment-and-alcohol/licensing-act/live-music-act-2012).
That's the pub's to arrange, not the receptionist's.

#### Hire the room upstairs (Should)

**Caller:** Someone planning a birthday, a wake, a christening or a work do

**The receptionist:**
- A message for the manager with the date, how many, the occasion, and
  whether they want food.
- No price is quoted unless the manager has set one, and nothing is
  promised.

#### Christmas parties (Could)

**Caller:** Office organisers, families

**The receptionist:**
- The manager's Christmas menu and dates, if set.
- A message for the manager to arrange the deposit and pre-order (left
  for later; see Decisions).

**Rules:** A deposit should be a small part of the price, and any
cancellation charge should reflect what the pub actually loses (CMA:
https://www.gov.uk/government/news/wedding-venues-advised-to-play-fair).

### Food and drink

#### What's on tap? (Should)

**Caller:** Ale drinkers

**The receptionist:** Today's list from the back office ("Today"). An ale
that isn't on the list is never said to be on.

#### Is the kitchen still serving? (Must)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** The food times, today's: 12pm to 9pm, or roasts
until 6pm on Sundays. Drinks are served until close.

#### Allergens (Must)

**Caller:** Customers with allergies, parents

**The receptionist:**
- Only from the menu's allergen information, with the kitchen's
  cross-contact warning.
- Never "safe".

**Rules:** For food not prepacked, allergen information must be available,
with a conversation (the FSA's best practice, March 2025), as for the café.

#### Gluten-free beer (Should)

**Caller:** Coeliacs

**The receptionist:**
- "Gluten-free" only for a beer the manager has marked so (the labelled
  bottle).
- Ordinary beer, lager and ale contain barley or wheat.
- Food follows the café's rule: "made without gluten ingredients", in a
  kitchen that uses flour, unless it's marked gluten-free.

**Rules:** A beer may be called gluten-free only at 20 ppm of gluten or
less, and one brewed from barley must still say "contains barley"
(Coeliac UK: https://www.coeliac.org.uk/frequently-asked-questions/gfbeer).

#### Vegan, vegetarian and alcohol-free (Should)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** From the menu: the nut roast, the vegan dishes, the
alcohol-free beer and soft drinks.

#### Sold out today (Should)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** What the manager has switched off on "Today" (the
roast beef, an ale) is gone today, and what's left is offered.

#### Drink prices (Could)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** From the menu only, never guessed.

#### Half pints, small glasses of wine, and tap water (Could)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** Yes: half pints, 25ml spirits and 125ml glasses of
wine are always available, and tap water is free.

**Rules:** These are mandatory conditions of every licence to sell alcohol
for drinking on the premises (the Licensing Act 2003 Mandatory Licensing
Conditions:
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2010/9780111491553). Draught beer and
cider are sold only in thirds, halves, two-thirds or multiples of a half
(Weights and Measures (Intoxicating Liquor) Order 1988:
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1988/2039).

#### Food to take away (Could)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** Off by default (see Decisions). If the manager turns
it on, the café's collection orders.

#### Bring our own cake or wine? (Could)

**Caller:** Someone celebrating

**The receptionist:** The manager's answer (a cake is fine; no own drinks).

### Age and the law

#### A 16 or 17-year-old drinking with a meal (Should)

**Caller:** Parents

**The receptionist:**
- With a table meal, a 16 or 17-year-old may have beer, wine or cider, if
  an adult with them buys it.
- Never spirits, never at the bar, and never bought by them.
- Staff check ID.

**Rules:** Licensing Act 2003 s149 and s150
(https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/150).

#### What ID do you accept? (Should)

**Caller:** Young adults, parents

**The receptionist:**
- Challenge 25: anyone who looks under 25 is asked.
- A passport, a photo driving licence, a PASS card, or since 15 September
  2026 a certified digital ID app, if the pub takes them.
- It's the staff's call on the night, never promised on the phone.

**Rules:**
- Every licensed premises must have an age verification policy asking for
  photo ID with a hologram or ultraviolet mark from anyone who looks under
  18, or an older age the policy sets (the mandatory conditions, as above).
- From 15 September 2026, certified digital ID can be accepted in England
  and Wales. It's the pub's choice
  (https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-rules-pave-the-way-for-businesses-to-adopt-digital-proof-of-age-for-alcohol-sales).

#### An 18th, or a party with under-18s (Could)

**Caller:** Parents

**The receptionist:** A message for the manager, who arranges it. Never
promises alcohol for anyone under 18.

#### Serving someone who's had a lot to drink (Could)

**Caller:** A caller saying a friend is drunk, or asking to be let in

**The receptionist:** Nothing is promised: it's the staff's call. If anyone
is unwell or at risk, 999.

**Rules:** Selling alcohol to someone who is drunk is an offence (Licensing
Act 2003 s141: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/141).

### People and access

#### Dogs (Should)

As "With a dog", above, for anyone asking without booking.

#### Access, toilets and parking (Should)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** The manager's answers: step-free, an accessible
toilet, the nearest car park.

#### Smoking and vaping (Could)

**Caller:** Anyone

**The receptionist:** Smoking only in the beer garden. Vaping by the
manager's rule (the garden only, by default).

**Rules:** Smoking is banned in enclosed and substantially enclosed public
places, so a shelter must be at least half open (Health Act 2006:
https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Environmental-Health/Smokefree-Regulations-and-shelters.pdf).
Vaping isn't covered by that law; it's the pub's own rule.

#### Babies and children (Could)

**Caller:** Parents

**The receptionist:**
- High chairs, baby changing and the children's menu, from the manager's
  answers.
- Breastfeeding is welcome anywhere.

**Rules:** A pub must not treat a woman less favourably because she is
breastfeeding (Equality Act 2010 s17).

### After the visit

#### Left something behind (Should)

**Caller:** Customer

**The receptionist:** A message for the team, with what and when. Never
says it's been found unless the back office says so.

#### Unhappy with the food or service (Should)

**Caller:** Customer

**The receptionist:** Sorry, and a message for the manager. Never promises
a refund.

#### Felt unwell after eating (Should)

**Caller:** Customer

**The receptionist:** As the café's:
- sorry;
- their GP or NHS 111, or 999 if it's serious;
- a message for the manager today;
- no fault and no guess at the cause.

#### A reaction happening now (Should)

**Caller:** Someone frightened

**The receptionist:** 999 first, before anything else, as the takeaway's
and the café's.

#### A neighbour complaining about noise (Could)

**Caller:** A neighbour

**The receptionist:**
- Sorry, and a message for the manager: urgent if it's happening now.
- Never argues, never promises the music stops.

**Rules:** Preventing public nuisance is one of the four licensing
objectives. A complaint is the manager's to answer.

### Other callers

#### A member of staff ringing in sick (Could)

**The receptionist:** An urgent message for the manager.

#### Suppliers, deliveries, job applicants, sales calls (Could)

**The receptionist:**
- A message for the manager (no account details).
- The manager's careers answer for jobs.
- A polite no thank you to sales calls.

#### Can you call me a taxi? (Could)

**The receptionist:** The local firm's number from the manager's answers.
It never books one.

### Hours and information

#### Opening hours, last orders and bank holidays (Must)

**The receptionist:** The week's hours, food times, and any closures or
changed hours.

#### Card, cash and tips (Could)

**The receptionist:** Card and cash, with no charge for card. Tips go to
the team.

**Rules:**
- A card surcharge is banned (Payment Surcharges Regulations 2012, as
  amended).
- Tips must go to the workers, fairly (Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act
  2023:
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/distributing-tips-fairly-statutory-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-on-fair-and-transparent-distribution-of-tips-html-version).

## What the demo needs beyond the restaurant and the café

- **Areas with their own rules**: the restaurant's areas. The bar's tables
  are bookable, but not during big matches. Dogs are welcome everywhere, so
  there's no dog rule to keep.
- **Children's hours**: a booking with children ends by 9pm.
- **What's on**: the manager's list of events and fixtures, with times and
  where. A new tool answers "are you showing the match?" from it.
- **The quiz as a booking**: a team booking at a fixed time on one day,
  with a price a head (the café's afternoon tea, at a fixed weekly time).
- **"Today" with what's on tap**: the café's "Today" (sold out and a
  notice), plus today's ales.
- **Age and ID answers** in the knowledge, as the law is.
- **Allergens, the reaction script and after-the-visit messages**, as the
  café's.
- **Guardrails**:
  - no time nothing gave (`invented_time`);
  - no price nothing gave (`invented_price`);
  - never "safe" for an allergy;
  - "gluten-free" only for marked food and drink;
  - no fixture nothing listed (a new `invented_fixture`);
  - no refund promised;
  - no card surcharge;
  - no health advice or guess at the cause.

## Checked for gaps

- **A fixture is only what the manager listed.** "We usually show it" is
  never said: a promised match the pub doesn't show is the commonest
  complaint.
- **First come means no promise**, for the garden and the bar.
- **The children's hour is the pub's policy**, said as such, never "by
  law".
- **Drink with a meal at 16 or 17 is the law said plainly**, and still the
  staff's call on the night: the receptionist never promises anyone will
  be served.
- **ID is never promised to be enough**: digital ID is the pub's choice.
- **Dogs are welcome everywhere**, and assistance dogs always, whatever the
  manager later sets.
- **"Gluten-free" beer is only the labelled one.**
- **The room upstairs is a message**, never a price or a promise.
- **A reaction happening now is 999 first.**
